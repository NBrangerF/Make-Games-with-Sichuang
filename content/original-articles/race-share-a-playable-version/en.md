# Pack this version for the next player

One friend receives a board image; another receives instructions for reroll tokens. Both think they are discussing the same version. Even if you have chosen C in your own mind, the games on their tables may differ. Before sharing, bring rules, materials, and version together.

We are sharing The 24-Space Race C v0.1: two players, a choice between two dice, twenty-four spaces, four transfers, and an exact finish. The previous chapter’s complete rules are ready to use. Put them with the board and write an invitation your friend can act on.

## Make every component point to the same game

A minimal set contains the board, full instructions, and a list asking for two pawns and two ordinary six-sided dice. Distinguishable paper pieces can replace pawns. The board should show 0 and 24, transfers 3 → 11, 8 → 17, 14 → 4, and 22 → 12, and an understandable number order.

Mark both rules and board C v0.1. Exclude reroll tokens, replenishment reminders, and the ten-round cooperative deadline. If you want to show the investigation, put B, R, E, and S in a separate comparison sheet identified as other experiments. Recipients should not need the whole course to guess which rules apply to their setup.

## Write an invitation someone can use

Try this: “I’ve made a two-player paper race and haven’t playtested it yet. Could you try setting up from this sheet? Circle any step that is unclear and note whether you asked someone for help. Afterwards, I’d like to hear what you thought of choosing a die.”

For a local test, state that it begins at space 6 with preset dice, and do not claim to investigate full-game pacing. For complete play, you can record turns and elapsed time, defining when timing starts and stops. You can also ask whether someone wants another game instead of inferring lasting interest from laughter.

## Put the change beside the version number

Before sending it, check the key moves yourself: choosing 2 from 6 reaches 17; choosing 2 from 20 reaches 12; choosing 2 from 23 leaves the pawn still. In the invitation, name what you still want to learn, such as whether setup works without help or the waits feel too long. Recipients then know where feedback would be useful.

Suppose a later revision only clarifies that passing does not activate a space. You might label it C v0.2, noting a communication change without changing the board or play. Replacing exact finishing with reaching or passing is a rules change and needs a new ending clause. “Improved the experience” alone does not let someone reproduce the difference.

## Keep the drafts—you can take them further

To keep exploring shared resources and divided work, switch to the complete river path. There, collecting a plank can change whether your partner can advance. Here, choosing a die changes your own position in the race. The two boards give you different design questions to follow.

From that first slide down a snake to this set of materials, you have made something you can keep revising. Find two people, set out the board, and start with these rules. A useful clue for the next draft may arrive when someone picks up the dice and pauses to ask a question.

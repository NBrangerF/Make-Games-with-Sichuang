# Why keep only the two dice?

Several drafts are on the table: base B, choice C, reroll R, supply E, and cooperation S. You are about to give a friend one game to try. Which rules go in the bag, and which stay in the notebook?

We will pack choice version C first: the same twenty-four-space board, two people, two dice each turn, one selected result, and one move. The base rules otherwise stay the same. This is the draft we are preparing for playtesting.

## Return to the small decision you wanted

Our provisional intention is to give people familiar with roll-and-move a small, understandable decision. At space 6 with results 2 and 6, destinations 17 and 12 connect reading the dice to checking the landing. C needs no extra resource and no additional decision about spending a token before moving and then having to accept a new result.

That choice leaves questions to watch. Some rolls offer no difference between the dice, and some people may prefer to pick up a die and move straight away. During play, notice whether the pause to choose makes the route worth studying or simply adds a wait.

## Explain what the omitted rules examine

The reroll version asks when to spend another chance. The supply version asks how those chances return. The cooperation version changes who succeeds together. Once their purposes are written down, you can leave this draft with the rules it needs.

Camel Up uses betting to shift attention from controlling a personal pawn to judging race outcomes. It offers a rich direction, but requires different rewards and information judgments. An elegant mechanism in a published game still needs to be assessed against the invitation of our own design.

## What can you check before packing?

Rules inspection can establish that choosing 2 from 6 reaches 17 in C, doubles still produce only one move, and two overshooting results from 23 leave the pawn still. We can also check whether the supplied materials actually state these clauses.

You can walk through these details before the handoff. Leave time for friends to show whether they can read the instructions alone, handle the pieces comfortably, and want to spend time choosing. Turn order also needs investigation; identical dice do not settle its effect.

## Leave a reason you can return to

Write a C version note: keep two-dice selection, the four transfers, exact finishing, and alternating turns. Exclude tokens, replenishment, a cooperative deadline, and extra turns. Record the reason as “first examine whether the small choice can be understood,” followed by independent setup and a local landing task.

When feedback comes back, put it beside that reason. If choosing creates hesitation without making the route more interesting, reconsider. A written reason helps you see what you are responding to and explain a changed direction to someone designing with you.

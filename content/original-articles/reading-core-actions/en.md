# What Actions Turn “Help the Animals Cross” into a Game?

“Help the animals cross the river” tells us what we want to accomplish, but not how players do it. Picking up Rabbit and placing him on the far bank would complete the crossing while skipping the game. Designing actions means turning a story’s intention into changes that are allowed on the table.

We will first make a paper draft of the complete base game, then examine why its actions need these details. Drawing lines, writing labels, and moving pieces are enough to start. Its appearance can change as the rules become clearer.

## Put the pieces of a whole game on the table

Draw four connected spaces in order: the starting bank, the first stepping stone, the second stepping stone, and the far bank. Make a Rabbit piece, a Hedgehog piece, twenty paper planks, and a round marker. Write rounds 1 to 4 and place the marker on round 1. Add a turn reminder pointing first to Lin so that players can keep track of whose turn it is.

Lin controls Rabbit; Yu controls Hedgehog. Both animals start on the starting bank. Put four planks in a central area labeled “Available planks,” and sixteen in a separate area labeled “Spare pieces.” Leave an empty area labeled “Spent planks.” Spare pieces represent new planks that have not been collected. Keep them separate from planks that have already been spent.

Play for up to four rounds. In each round, Lin takes one turn, then Yu takes one turn. On your turn, choose one of these three actions:

- **Move:** If at least one available plank remains, spend one and move your animal to the next adjacent space toward the far bank. You cannot move backward or skip a space. Put the plank in the spent area; it cannot be used again.
- **Collect:** If your animal is still on the starting bank, take two new planks from the spare area and add them to the shared available supply. Your animal stays where it is. You cannot also move this turn.
- **Wait:** Leave animals and planks unchanged and end your turn.

Animals may share a space and do not block each other. An animal on the far bank can only wait on later turns. Players may discuss their plans, but each decides their own animal’s action. As soon as both animals reach the far bank, the players win together. If they have not both arrived after both turns of round 4, they lose together. There are no personal scores, weather, or special abilities.

Four rounds allow at most eight collect actions, so sixteen spare pieces can represent all the new planks those actions could produce. The spare area supplies pieces for representing the game; it is not a stock the players already own. Putting all twenty planks in the center would change the game.

## Which details does an action need?

In round 1, Lin chooses to move. He checks that an available plank remains, puts one in the spent area, and moves Rabbit to the first stepping stone. The shared supply falls from four to three, and his turn ends. If Yu collects, she takes two planks from the spare area and adds them to the center, raising the supply to five. Hedgehog stays on the starting bank.

These actions work because we have specified who can perform them, when they are allowed, what they cost, and what changes afterward. “Movement costs planks” leaves too much unanswered: how many planks, how far, whether an animal can skip past Rabbit, and where the spent pieces go. A vivid action name cannot fill those gaps.

An action’s role may also depend on the longer journey. Collecting moves no animal, but can supply planks needed for later moves. It uses a turn and may leave the collecting animal too little time to arrive. Its value comes from its relationship with the crossing. Picking up two paper pieces does not have to feel thrilling on its own for the action to matter.

## What is a choice, and what carries it out?

When Lin chooses to move, he decides what to do. Putting the plank in the spent area carries out the required consequence. He cannot also choose to leave that plank available. Both involve his hands, but they serve different purposes.

In a tabletop game, players also maintain positions, resources, and the round. Missing an update can give the next player an incorrect situation.

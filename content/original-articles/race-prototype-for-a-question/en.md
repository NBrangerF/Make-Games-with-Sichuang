# What should one small board answer first?

You could spend an afternoon drawing beautiful snakes, or begin with two arrows: 8 to 17 and 14 to 4. Which is useful depends on the current question. If you want to know whether someone understands that only landing activates a ladder, detailed scales will not yet supply the answer.

Choose an action to check, then make the materials someone needs to carry it out. That is the prototype for now. It can be rough, provided the parts needed to answer the question are still there.

## Start by checking the die choice

“Is it more fun with a choice?” contains several questions. Seeing both outcomes, noticing the ladder, taking time to compare, and wanting another game after losing require different observations. Begin with one: can someone new to C understand that two rolled dice offer one selected move, and work out each candidate result?

Set the pawn on 6 and the dice to 2 and 6. Keep the full 24-space numbering and all four transfers. Provide the C action reminder, then ask the participant to choose and move. Do not announce that the 2 travels farther. Someone who chooses 6 and correctly moves to 12 may understand the rules perfectly, even without making the choice we expected.

## Fix the result to examine the relationship

Setting out 2 and 6 saves waiting for that roll to happen. When you change a reminder card or ladder drawing, use the same results again. You can then compare how the materials explain the same action.

This small task is useful for checking instructions. To learn how often a game offers an interesting choice, or whether players enjoy its pace, arrange a separate full game. Keeping those tasks apart stops one position diagram from having to answer for the entire design.

## What can be removed, and what must remain?

This test needs no reroll tokens, replenishment space, or cooperative deadline. Those belong to R, E, and S and introduce other questions. Understanding a landing requires seeing space 6, the intervening spaces, ladder base 8, and ladder top 17. Label the material as one situation on a complete board, rather than implying that this fragment is the entire game.

Cutting out only cards marked 6, 8, and 17 might accidentally teach the advance from 6 to 8 as a single jump. After removing a component, inspect whether its relationship disappeared too. A simple prototype should clarify a question, not depend on the facilitator silently filling missing rules.

## Make room on the recording sheet

Leave columns for the die selected, the destination reached, and any pause. If someone adds the dice, record the action and check the cue. If they climb while passing 8, circle that position and review the trigger instruction. Finding where the difficulty occurs gives the revision somewhere to start.

Leave space for something you did not anticipate. Your possible explanations help you watch, but a friend may read the materials another way. With this sheet and the board, you can arrange the first try.

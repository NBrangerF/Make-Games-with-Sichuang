# Radlands: Why Does One Water Matter More a Turn Earlier?

In Radlands, water pays for people, events, and abilities. As you target the opponent’s three camps, check who stands in front and how soon an event will arrive. Spending the same water now or later can change whether the opponent gets a chance to repair.

This article uses the base-game portion of the official English V2.1 rules and only the original 34 camps, excluding *Cult of Chrome*. The current [Roxley product page](https://roxley.com/products/radlands-complete) presents Complete packaging that includes the expansion. Its download entry is dated November 13, 2024, but the actual file identifies V2.1 on page 3 and ©2026 on its back cover. These are different dates for different things.

The three comparisons below are our original local constructions, not complete game histories or optimal strategies. In each, both players have already lost two camps and retain only the camp specified below, making the ending easier to follow. Unlisted people, hands, and queues are empty. Unless specified otherwise, each player's Water Silo and Raiders are in their respective table areas, outside the hand and event queue. The deck has enough cards without reshuffling, and cards in the discard pile do not participate. Specified draws make the examples reproducible; players do not know the deck's order. Other abilities remain unused, and no traits modify these costs or timing.

## Angle one: Store a future exchange, not an unspent water disc

A retains an undamaged Garage; B retains an undamaged Supply Depot. Near the end of A's Actions phase, A has one water left and only Bombardment in hand, with Water Silo still on the table. Bombardment costs four water and has event time 3; both queues are empty. Compare buying and retaining Water Silo with ending immediately. [Card image and Water Silo rules, pp.7, 11](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0246/2190/8043/files/Radlands_Rulebook_Booklet_2.1.pdf?v=1784906929)

Fix the same response in both branches. During B's Replenish phase, B draws Cult Leader and takes three water, then chooses no actions and returns all water. On A's next turn, A handles the empty event queue, draws Gunner, and takes three water. Ordinary water cannot carry over; the starting player's one-water first-turn exception is already past. [Turn sequence, p.9](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0246/2190/8043/files/Radlands_Rulebook_Booklet_2.1.pdf?v=1784906929)

| Branch | A's last water on the previous turn | Available before payment next turn | Can A play the four-water event by this route? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Store | Pay one to take Water Silo into hand | Three water, plus one from junking Water Silo, totaling four | Yes: pay four and place Bombardment in space 3 |
| Do not store | Return it at turn end | Three water | No: retain Bombardment, end, and return three water |

The stored branch ends with only Gunner in A's hand and zero water. Water Silo is back on the table; Bombardment remains queued and has not resolved. The other branch leaves A with two cards and an empty queue. Both remaining camps are undamaged, so play continues. This comparison does not include additional draws, junk effects, or other abilities that could provide water.

Storing has not created free income: A paid one on the earlier turn and exchanged it for one later. It changes which turn can concentrate the spending, allowing a three-water replenishment to cross a four-water threshold. Buying and immediately junking Water Silo now would produce only 3−1+1=3 water, leaving the shortfall unchanged. Repeated access does not imply repeated profit.

When designing storage for an original game, write down a cost above the normal replenishment amount, then compare whether it becomes payable with storage. Separately ask whether storage consumes a current opportunity, whether it consumes a card, and whether the container returns after redemption. Water Silo stays in hand and can be bought again after returning to the table. Those state changes are more precise than simply saying that surplus can be saved.

## Angle two: Front positions decide who stays protected and who loses an ability

Use a new snapshot. A retains an undamaged Railgun. B retains an undamaged Supply Depot with a healthy Repair Bot in front, played on an earlier turn. Near the end of B's Actions phase, B has zero water and only Magnus Karv in hand. B junks that card, using its junk icon to take a facedown punk from the deck's top, without paying Magnus Karv's play cost. A punk has no abilities. One damage destroys it and returns it facedown to the top of the deck without anyone looking at its face. [Icons and people, pp.6, 8, 10](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0246/2190/8043/files/Radlands_Rulebook_Booklet_2.1.pdf?v=1784906929)

The column previously contained one person, so the new punk can go either in front of or behind Repair Bot. Either arrangement reaches the limit of two people per column. B ends. A then handles an empty queue, draws Gunner, takes three water, and pays two to use the ready Railgun, dealing one ordinary damage to the front person in this column. [Railgun image, p.5; protection and damage, pp.7–8; readiness, p.11](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0246/2190/8043/files/Radlands_Rulebook_Booklet_2.1.pdf?v=1784906929)

| Front to back, viewed from the opponent | Damage resolution | B's position when A ends |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Punk→Repair Bot→camp | Destroy the punk and return it to the deck's top | One person remains: a healthy Repair Bot; the camp is still protected |
| Repair Bot→punk→camp | Turn Repair Bot sideways as damaged; leave it in place | Two people remain: damaged Repair Bot and the punk; the camp is still protected |

In both branches A returns the remaining one water and keeps Gunner. B has no hand, and Magnus Karv is in the discard pile. The second branch's punk remains in play. Neither remaining camp has been destroyed, so play continues.

Counting people makes the second branch look stronger; counting usable abilities shows what the first branch preserved. A damaged person cannot use abilities, whereas a damaged camp can still be ready. Repair Bot's activated restore ability costs two water. Whether it can help later also depends on having a legal damaged target: being healthy does not itself deliver a heal.

Protection does not automatically vanish when a person takes damage. In the second branch the damaged bot still stands at the front and shields the cards behind it, changing the target of the next ordinary hit. The two placements create the same punk from the same discarded card but assign future losses to different objects. In a positioning prototype, record position, damage, and available actions together. Counting objects alone misses changes in what those objects can do.

## Angle three: Resolving earlier removes a chance to repair

In the third comparison, A retains a healthy Garage. B retains a damaged Supply Depot with a punk in front, and has only Repair Bot in hand. A has just completed Replenish, holds only Magnus Karv, has three water, and has not used Garage. During this turn's Events phase, A's Raiders advanced from space 2 to space 1. No other events are queued.

Arriving in space 1 does not resolve an event again during that Events phase. The phase first resolves the event already in space 1, then moves the others. Garage costs one water to produce a raid effect. If Raiders is already in space 1, that raid resolves it immediately. Raiders makes the opponent choose one of their camps to damage, bypassing people protecting it. [Garage image, p.5; Raid and event order, p.9](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0246/2190/8043/files/Radlands_Rulebook_Booklet_2.1.pdf?v=1784906929)

| Branch | Sequence | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A spends one now | Garage triggers a raid; B must choose the last damaged camp; another damage destroys it | A wins immediately with two water unspent, without waiting for turn end |
| A waits for the normal countdown | A ends and returns three water; B draws Gunner and takes three water; B pays one to play Repair Bot behind the punk, restores the camp with its entry trait, and returns the remaining two water | On A's next turn, Raiders only changes the healthy camp to damaged; A has not won |

The delayed branch's Repair Bot has just entered play and cannot immediately use its two-water activated ability. The effect used here is its entry trait. The column has only a punk, leaving space behind it; the restore target is the camp, not Repair Bot itself. [Repair Bot image, p.6; entering play, restore, and readiness, pp.8, 10–11](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0246/2190/8043/files/Radlands_Rulebook_Booklet_2.1.pdf?v=1784906929)

Finish the delayed branch: Raiders returns to A's table area. A replenishes by drawing Looter and taking three water, then chooses to end, returning all three and retaining Magnus Karv and Looter. B retains Gunner; Repair Bot and the punk survive, and the last camp remains damaged. The immediate branch has already ended, so B cannot receive an extra turn to rescue it.

Acceleration did not add a hit: each route resolved Raiders exactly once. What changed was whether a restore happened before the hit. The public countdown therefore exposes a response window, and spending one water can change whether that window exists. This example fixes both B's possession of Repair Bot and the decision to restore. It does not show that every event should be accelerated or that opponents can always repair their camps.

## Self-checks with answers

1. Can buying and immediately junking Water Silo turn three water into four? **No. Paying one and gaining one leaves three.**
2. Does the damaged Repair Bot still protect cards behind it in angle two? **Yes. It remains at the front, although its person ability is unavailable.**
3. Does a punk hit by Railgun go to the discard pile? **No. Return it facedown to the deck's top without looking at its face.**
4. Does Raiders resolve in the same Events phase when moving from space 2 to space 1? **No. That phase's space-1 resolution happened before movement.**
5. Why can the newly played Repair Bot restore the camp in angle three? **Its entry trait does so; its two-water activated ability was not used.**

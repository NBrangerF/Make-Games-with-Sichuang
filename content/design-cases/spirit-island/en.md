# Spirit Island: Why Can the Position Improve Without Destroying an Enemy?

Push an Invader next door and a land may avoid a future Build. Help Dahan survive an attack and they can strike back. In Spirit Island, a small move or a defence can keep affecting later steps. Counting only the pieces removed now misses that reach.

This article uses the English base rulebook linked from the [publisher's product page](https://shop.greaterthangames.com/products/spirit-island): 32 pages, with a 2016–2018 copyright on p.28 and no explicit printing identified. It excludes expansions, Adversaries, Scenarios, the thematic map, and Blight Cards. We also apply the [official FAQ's starting-Blight correction](https://querki.net/raw/darker/spirit-island-faq/Setup): the pool starts with 5 times the player count, plus 1, giving six Blight solo or eleven with two players. This is one extra overall, not one extra per player. Initial Blight on the Island comes separately from the box.

The three independent situations are our original local states, not complete histories reconstructed from setup or observed playtests. Each Spirit has legally completed Growth, obtained the relevant cards, and has sufficient Card Plays; no unlisted Powers intervene. Other lands resolve normally without ending the game early or changing the specified lands. Each example states its stopping point, distinguishing a local success from winning the game.

## Angle one: Moving one Explorer can prevent a Town from being built

Two players use River Surges in Sunlight and Lightning's Swift Strike, shortened here to River and Lightning. Adjacent Jungle J and Mountain M contain no Blight, Dahan, or buildings. J has one Explorer and one River Presence; M has no Invaders. River also has Presence elsewhere, and Lightning remains present on the Island. The current cards show Ravage in Wetlands and Build in Jungles; this turn's subsequent Explore reveals Sands. None of those actions occurs in M. A City elsewhere prevents clearing J from satisfying the current Terror Level I victory condition.

Each Spirit has one Energy and plays just the following card, paying one Energy and retaining zero. River's **Wash Away** is Slow, Range 1, and can Push up to three Explorers/Towns from its target. River's Presence in J puts it at Range 0, within that limit. Lightning's **Lightning's Boon** is Fast and targets any Spirit. It targets River here, allowing up to two of River's Slow Powers to be used as Fast this turn. Neither Spirit uses an Innate Power in this example. [Wash Away, original card image](https://spiritislandwiki.com/index.php?title=Wash_Away); [Lightning's Boon, original card image](https://spiritislandwiki.com/index.php?title=Lightning%27s_Boon)

Compare accepting the faster timing with remaining Slow. Both branches have already paid the stated costs:

| Timing | Use Wash Away as Fast | Keep Wash Away Slow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fast Power Phase | Lightning resolves its support first; River Pushes J's Explorer into adjacent M. | Lightning resolves its support, but River declines the optional acceleration and moves nothing yet. |
| Invader Build | Empty J does not Build. M is not a Jungle and does not Build. | J has an Explorer and zero Towns and Cities, so it adds one Town. |
| End of the Slow Power Phase | J is empty; M has one Explorer. Wash Away has already been used and cannot be used again. | River Pushes J's Explorer and new Town together into M. Two pieces fit the limit of three, leaving J empty. |

Exploring Sands adds nothing to J or M; exploration elsewhere does not alter this two-land account. Neither route generates Fear or destroys a piece. Yet the Slow branch contains one extra Town. It remains in M and will be affected normally when a later action reaches it. Pushing does not erase a piece from the game. [Build and phase order, rules pp.8–10; single use and Push, pp.17, 19](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/5wzghwnbsi39msyy6vvox/Spirit-Island-CORE-Rulebook.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=86i7aofqbzhulezjr7z0ssyff&st=iqv6wpml)

The design lesson is to count interrupting future production among an action's benefits. Acceleration does not increase the movement allowance here; it empties J before the Build condition is checked. This does not establish that Fast Powers are always better. Lightning spends an Energy and a Card Play, and M receives a threat. Looking only at the empty J misses both that cost and the neighboring stock of Invaders. An Adversary allowing construction in empty lands could also invalidate the inference.

## Angle two: Dahan are not shields that absorb damage for the land

Start a different solo River game. Wetland W is about to Ravage. It contains one Explorer, one Town, one City, two undamaged Dahan, and one River Presence, with no Blight. River has Presence elsewhere and there is another City elsewhere on the Island. Four Blight remain in the pool. All four Fear markers remain in their pool, and no earned Fear Card awaits resolution this turn.

River previously acquired the Minor Power **Sap the Strength of Multitudes** legally. It plays only that card this turn, spending zero Energy and one Card Play, and targets W during the Fast Phase. Its base effect is Defend 5 at Range 0. River's Presence in W satisfies targeting without needing the Air threshold that increases Range. [Original card and targeting diagram, rules p.16; Defend, p.18](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/5wzghwnbsi39msyy6vvox/Spirit-Island-CORE-Rulebook.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=86i7aofqbzhulezjr7z0ssyff&st=iqv6wpml)

The Invaders total 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 Damage. Defend reduces it to 1. **The land takes 1 and the Dahan collectively take 1.** Damage does not first pass through the Dahan, leaving a remainder for the land. The land is below the threshold of 2, so it gains no Blight. One Dahan takes one Damage but survives. Both Dahan therefore counterattack, totaling four Damage.

Allocate three Damage to the City and one to the Explorer, destroying both exactly. The Town remains undamaged. Destroying the City generates two Fear; the Explorer generates none. Two of the four Fear markers move to Generated Fear, without earning a card. The Blight pool stays at four, River's Presence remains, and both Dahan survive. [Damage, counterattack, and Health, rules pp.9, 15–16](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/5wzghwnbsi39msyy6vvox/Spirit-Island-CORE-Rulebook.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=86i7aofqbzhulezjr7z0ssyff&st=iqv6wpml)

Return to the same state and decline the card's effect instead. Six Damage hits the land and Dahan simultaneously. The land gains one Blight, reducing the pool from four to three and destroying River's Presence in W. There was no previous Blight, so none cascades. Two Damage destroys each Dahan; the six cannot be spread to keep them alive. With no survivors, there is no counterattack. All three Invader pieces remain and no Fear is generated. River's Presence elsewhere prevents defeat from losing all Presence.

The turn's subsequent Build is in Jungles and Explore in Sands, leaving W unchanged, and no other Power affects it. During Time Passes, the wounded Dahan in the defended branch heals and Defend expires. Protection here both limits losses and preserves the source of a counterattack. The defense card itself did not deal those four Damage. With no Dahan in W, the same Defend 5 would only reduce incoming harm, without automatically clearing Invaders. Also distinguish reducing Ravage Damage to zero from skipping Ravage: the former still allows a Dahan counterattack; the latter does not produce that counterattack.

## Angle three: Fear changes the destination as well as providing rewards

The third solo River game begins at the Fast Power Phase. Across the entire Island there are exactly one undamaged Town and three Explorers, with no Cities. The Town occupies Coastal land C, where River has Presence; River also has Presence elsewhere. Terror Level is still I. The base Fear deck uses groups of 3/3/3. Its first two cards have already been earned and resolved, so earning one more reveals the Terror Level II divider. Three of the four Fear markers are in Generated Fear, with just one remaining in the pool. Four Blight remain, the Invader deck is not empty, and no other ending condition is triggered.

During the Spirit Phase, River had two Energy and played only **Flash Floods**, paying two and retaining zero. It is Fast with Range 1. River's Presence in C makes the target legal. The Coastal condition supplies the additional Damage, giving 1 + 1 = 2. Apply it all to the Town, destroying it exactly. [Flash Floods, original card image](https://spiritislandwiki.com/index.php?title=Flash_Floods)

Destroying the Town generates one Fear. Progress goes from 3/4 to 4/4; the top Fear Card moves to the earned area and all four markers return to the Fear Pool. The newly exposed Terror Level II takes effect immediately. With no Towns or Cities anywhere, its victory condition is met. The three Explorers can remain on the Island: victory does not wait for the next Fear step to reveal the newly earned card. [Fear-deck setup, rules p.6; victory and Terror diagram, p.12](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/5wzghwnbsi39msyy6vvox/Spirit-Island-CORE-Rulebook.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=86i7aofqbzhulezjr7z0ssyff&st=iqv6wpml)

Change only the initial Fear progress to 2/4. All other conditions and actions remain the same. The Town is still destroyed, but Fear reaches only 3/4. No new card is earned and Terror Level remains I. Three surviving Explorers prevent the “no Invaders anywhere” condition from being met, so the Power ends without victory. This is not defeat; play continues.

Both branches remove the same piece. The difference comes from global Fear progress and the approaching divider. Describing Fear in a prototype only as “fill a track to earn a reward” would omit its role in changing which enemies need clearing. Nor is “remove all Towns” a constant victory condition: it needs the appropriate Terror Level and there must be no Cities anywhere.

## The designer's account and our analysis

In his signed design notes on rulebook p.29, R. Eric Reuss recalls questioning why a colonization game had abstracted away the perspective of existing inhabitants. He wanted to build a game from a position of resisting colonization, choosing an imagined conflict rather than modeling one particular historical war. [“Design Notes”](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/5wzghwnbsi39msyy6vvox/Spirit-Island-CORE-Rulebook.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=86i7aofqbzhulezjr7z0ssyff&st=iqv6wpml)

That is the designer's recollection. Our analysis is that a changed viewpoint still needs rules defining what deserves protection and what counts as driving a threat away. Land, Dahan, and Fear perform different parts of that work above. These calculations do not demonstrate that the game eliminates dominant-player problems in cooperation, nor do they validate a model of real colonial history.

## Self-checks with answers

1. Why do angle one's results differ when J ends empty both times? **The Slow branch has already built a Town. It moves to M instead of disappearing.**
2. After accelerating Wash Away, may River use it again in the Slow Phase? **No. Acceleration changes timing, not the number of uses.**
3. Do Dahan absorb the one remaining Damage in angle two so the land takes none? **No. Both receive one; the land avoids Blight because its Damage is below two.**
4. Why does a wounded Dahan still counterattack for two Damage? **It has not reached its two-Health destruction threshold. Each surviving Dahan deals two, a separate value from remaining Health.**
5. Why can angle three end with three Explorers still present? **It has just reached Terror Level II, requiring no Towns or Cities. At Level I, that position would not suffice.**

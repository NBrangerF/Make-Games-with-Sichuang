# The Crew: Why Can Winning a Trick Make the Whole Team Lose?

In *The Crew: The Quest for Planet Nine*, a high card can secure a target or take it away from the teammate who needs it. Designing cooperation involves more than adding individual scores together. Who receives a success, what others can know, and when success happens all matter.

This article uses the Thames & Kosmos English base rules, product 691868, document code 691868-02-060320, excluding *Mission Deep Sea*. The three independent deals and action sequences below are our original constructions, not rulebook exercises or observed playtests. All use four players, clockwise A→B→C→D, without a distress signal or additional communication restrictions. Each begins after dealing. A holds rocket 4, chooses a task first, and leads the opening trick. Small task cards are separate from the 40 playing cards: blue, green, yellow, and pink each run from 1 to 9, plus rockets 1–4. [Rules, pp.2, 8–10](https://www.thamesandkosmos.com/manuals/full/691868_Crew_Manual.pdf)

The full hands let readers verify the arithmetic. At the table, players see their own hands, public tasks, and information legally made public; they cannot discuss these all-seeing tables. Captured tricks are not a freely searchable archive either: only the most recent trick may be inspected. In each table, “blue 8, 9” means two separate blue cards. Every row contains exactly ten playing cards.

## Angle one: A cooperative objective changes what a high card is for

Use published mission 2: two tasks without order tokens. This attempt draws blue 2 and green 3. A selects blue 2 first; B takes green 3. [Logbook PDF p.3, left-hand mission 2, printed p.4](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/dd/5a/3a-the-crew-the-quest-for-planet-nine-log-book.pdf)

| Player | Complete starting hand |
| --- | --- |
| A | Rocket 4; blue 8, 9; green 2; yellow 1, 4, 7; pink 2, 5, 8 |
| B | Rocket 1; blue 2, 5; green 4, 7; yellow 2, 5; pink 3, 6, 9 |
| C | Rocket 2; blue 1, 4; green 3, 5, 6; yellow 3, 6; pink 1, 4 |
| D | Rocket 3; blue 3, 6, 7; green 1, 8, 9; yellow 8, 9; pink 7 |

The first trick begins A green 2→B green 7→C green 3. D now has three greens and must follow green; rocket 3 is unavailable. D is not obliged to beat green 7. [Following suit and rockets, rules pp.3–4](https://www.thamesandkosmos.com/manuals/full/691868_Crew_Manual.pdf)

- D plays green 1: B's green 7 wins all four cards, including B's required green 3. B turns that task face down.
- Return to the same decision and choose green 9: D captures green 3 instead of its task owner B. The mission attempt fails immediately; later tricks cannot repair it. [Task resolution, rules p.10](https://www.thamesandkosmos.com/manuals/full/691868_Crew_Manual.pdf)

Continue the green-1 branch. B won the previous trick and therefore leads: B blue 2→C blue 4→D blue 6→A blue 9. Everyone has blue and follows legally. A captures blue 2, completing the second task. The team succeeds and stops immediately. Eight cards have been captured; each player has eight left, totaling 32. They need not play those remaining cards.

D gives up a trick to help B complete a task; B then puts blue 2 into a trick A can win. Ranking their contributions by tricks won would miss their relationship. The task connects a card's value to a particular recipient, while the location of retained high cards changes the next opportunity for cooperation.

This sequence establishes that the stated actions can succeed. It does not establish that players will discover them with hidden information, or that D should always save high cards. Change task ownership, remaining suits, or the leader, and green 9 might be exactly the card that saves the attempt.

## Angle two: Revealing a card also restricts what it can say

Start another attempt at mission 2. A chooses green 2 first; B takes yellow 4. Here is the complete new deal, with B's communication still unused:

| Player | Complete starting hand |
| --- | --- |
| A | Rocket 4; blue 2, 5; green 6, 9; yellow 5, 9; pink 1, 4, 7 |
| B | Rockets 1, 2; blue 1, 4; green 1, 4; yellow 1, 7; pink 3, 6 |
| C | Blue 3, 6; green 2, 5, 8; yellow 2, 4; pink 2, 5, 8 |
| D | Rocket 3; blue 7, 8, 9; green 3, 7; yellow 3, 6, 8; pink 9 |

After distributing tasks but before the opening trick, B displays yellow 7 with the green communication token at its top, identifying B's highest yellow, and takes a reminder card. Yellow 7 remains the tenth playable card; the reminder supplies no extra play. Because B also holds yellow 1, placing the token in the middle to claim “only yellow” would be false. [Communication text and component diagrams, rules pp.5–7](https://www.thamesandkosmos.com/manuals/full/691868_Crew_Manual.pdf)

Follow this route. Arrows show the actual order of play:

| Trick | Plays and resolution |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | A yellow 9→B yellow 1→C yellow 2→D yellow 3. A wins, with no target captured. B has nine playable cards left, including the displayed yellow 7. |
| 2 | A yellow 5→B yellow 7→C yellow 4→D yellow 6. B captures yellow 4 and completes that task. Playing yellow 7 removes the reminder from B's hand and turns the communication token red. |
| 3 | B green 1→C green 2→D green 3→A green 9. A completes green 2 and the team succeeds. Each player has seven cards left: 28 remaining plus 12 captured. |

After trick 1, yellow 7 has become B's only yellow, but the token must stay at the top. After playing it in trick 2, B cannot communicate another card. Communication is limited to once per player per attempt and occurs only before a trick, never during one. The marker preserves the original declaration; it does not continuously update the whole hand. [Rules pp.5, 7](https://www.thamesandkosmos.com/manuals/full/691868_Crew_Manual.pdf)

The signal rules out B holding yellow 8 or 9. It does not promise yellow 7 will win. In trick 2, D still holds yellow 8: choosing it instead would send yellow 4 to D and fail the mission. The marker does not let B transmit a complete set of future instructions, and one statement does not expose every uncommunicated card.

A beginning designer can examine three separate questions here: what may be disclosed, when disclosure happens, and whether it updates. The displayed yellow 7 reduces what needs remembering, the reminder points to a card still waiting to be played, and the red face records a spent allowance. These components have different jobs. Establishing whether they make learning easier for a particular group would require observing that group's handling errors; this article contains no such measurement.

## Angle three: The first completed task need not happen in the first trick

The third attempt uses published mission 3: two tasks with number tokens 1 and 2. This draw assigns token 1 to pink 2 and token 2 to blue 3. A chooses pink 2 with its token; B takes blue 3 with its token. Task 1 must precede task 2. Those numbers do not identify trick numbers. [Logbook PDF p.3, left-hand mission 3, printed p.4](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/dd/5a/3a-the-crew-the-quest-for-planet-nine-log-book.pdf); [rules pp.14–16](https://www.thamesandkosmos.com/manuals/full/691868_Crew_Manual.pdf)

| Player | Complete starting hand |
| --- | --- |
| A | Rocket 4; blue 1, 8; green 1, 4, 7; yellow 1, 4; pink 6, 9 |
| B | Rocket 1; blue 2, 9; green 2, 5; yellow 2, 5; pink 1, 4, 7 |
| C | Rocket 2; blue 3, 6; green 3, 6; yellow 3, 6; pink 2, 5, 8 |
| D | Rocket 3; blue 4, 5, 7; green 8, 9; yellow 7, 8, 9; pink 3 |

A opens with rocket 4; B, C, and D follow with rockets 1, 2, and 3. A wins the opening trick. No task is complete, but no ordering condition has been broken. All four rockets have left the hands, leaving 36 playing cards.

A next leads pink 9. B follows pink 1, C pink 2, and D pink 3. A captures pink 2, completing token 1. In trick 3, A plays blue 1, followed by B blue 9, C blue 3, and D blue 4. B captures blue 3, completing token 2. The team succeeds in the required order, 1→2. Twelve cards are captured and each player retains seven.

Now return to the end of the opening trick, preserving the deal. Replace trick 2 with A blue 1→B blue 9→C blue 3→D blue 4. B is the correct recipient of blue 3, but pink 2 with token 1 remains incomplete. The attempt therefore fails immediately. Planning to collect pink 2 later cannot repair a condition checked against the actual sequence.

A task can prescribe a route as well as a destination. An opening trick with no target can still change future conditions: this one removes every rocket while leaving A on lead. That does not prove that leading rocket 4 is optimal. Also, these two tasks require separate tricks because they belong to different players. Do not generalize that all ordered tasks must be separated: the rules also allow one player to complete consecutive order tokens in the same trick.

## The designer's account and our analysis

In an interview republished by IELLO on March 1, 2021, Thomas Sing recalled first imagining predetermined recipients for particular cards and testing the idea the following day. Communication was still unfinished that evening; he brought the subsequently adopted communication rule the next week. The space theme followed several weeks later. [Interview, “Acte 1 : de l’idée au prototype”](https://iello.fr/thomas-sing-nous-parle-de-the-crew/)

That is the designer's recollection of development. Our proposed exercise is to test whether changing the required recipient creates a need to cooperate, then separately test how information rules affect decisions. This is our inference, not a general design method supplied by Sing. His assessment of his early prototype does not establish that players have tested the three examples here.

## Self-checks with answers

1. Why can D play green 1 but not rocket 3 in angle one? **D still has greens and must follow green. Following suit does not require winning.**
2. Why does angle one succeed with 32 cards unplayed? **Both tasks have reached their respective owners; empty hands are not required.**
3. May B move yellow 7's marker to the middle after angle two's opening trick? **No. Although it is now B's only yellow, the original marker placement cannot update.**
4. Does “B's highest yellow is 7” guarantee that B wins trick 2? **No. D still has yellow 8; D chooses yellow 6 in this route.**
5. Why does completing no task in angle three's first trick avoid failure? **The numbers specify the order among tasks, not the trick in which each must be completed.**

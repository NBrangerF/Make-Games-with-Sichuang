# Root: Why Does One Map Create Three Different Pressures?

The Marquise has wood but cannot spend it on a building. The Eyrie has warriors but may collapse because it cannot fulfil one card. The Alliance can lose its soldiers while leaving influence behind. To understand Root’s asymmetric design, look at where resources are, when actions become compulsory, and what a conflict leaves in place.

This article uses the base factions in the October 13, 2025 Law of Root. The [publisher’s product page](https://ledergames.com/products/root-a-game-of-woodland-might-and-right) credits Cole Wehrle with the design. These are three separate snapshots in a four-player base-game setting; the Vagabond does not participate in these passages. No expansions, dominance victories or additional card effects apply. Letters temporarily name clearings. We specify the connections and state needed here, without reconstructing earlier play or presenting a new map or optimal strategy.

## Angle one: Enough wood does not mean you can pay

It is the Marquise’s Daylight, with two actions available. She has 8 points and three sawmills on the map. The next sawmill costs 3 wood and scores 3 points; see the track in [Learning to Play, page 11](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0106/0162/7706/files/Root_Base_Learn_to_Play_web_Oct_15_2020.pdf?v=1603389572). Clearing C, the proposed building site, has an open building slot.

Paths connect A—B—C, with no direct A–C connection. Other routes provide no alternative chain of clearings ruled by the Marquise. The only 3 wood on the entire map are at A. These three clearings contain no pieces beyond those listed, and no sympathy.

| Clearing | Marquise warriors / buildings | Eyrie warriors / buildings | Wood | Current ruler |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 1 / 1 sawmill | 0 / 0 | 3 | Marquise |
| B | 1 / 1 workshop | 2 / 0 | 0 | Eyrie |
| C | 3 / 0 | 0 / 0 | 0 | Marquise |

B is not ruled by the Marquise: warriors plus buildings give a 2–2 tie, which the Eyrie wins. A’s wood cannot cross B to pay for construction at C. Wood itself does not count toward rule. See [Law 2.5, 6.5.4 and 7.2.2, pages 3 and 6](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0106/0162/7706/files/Root_Base_Law_Oct_2025.pdf?v=1774903756).

Compare two choices. Path A stops without building and leaves the state unchanged. Path B spends one March action moving one cat from C to B. March allows up to two moves; only one is taken here. The move is legal because the Marquise rules its origin, C. B now holds two cats plus a workshop against two Eyrie warriors; C still has two cats. The Marquise now rules the entire A–B–C chain.

With the second action, she builds a sawmill at C, removes A’s 3 wood to pay and scores 3 points. The passage ends after that build resolves:

| Result | Wood at A | Cats at B / C | New building at C | Marquise points | Actions used here |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A: stop | 3 | 1 / 3 | None | 8 | 0 |
| B: move, then build | 0 | 2 / 2 | 1 sawmill | 11 | 2 |

No enemy was removed and no extra wood was produced. Resource access changed: reallocating one warrior turned existing stock into spendable stock. An interface showing only “3 total wood” would conceal the decision’s central condition.

In your own area game, distinguish total stock, usable stock and the actions needed to open access. Making wood a shared reserve available everywhere would remove this transport condition and change the importance of holding B. That changes more than a handling step. This example establishes immediate results; it does not prove another sawmill is always worth more than the troops, actions and wood retained by stopping.

## Angle two: A wild card buys flexibility and creates a future obligation

Move to the Eyrie’s Birdsong. Its leader is the Builder. The existing Decree is below; both Loyal Viziers count as bird cards. The hand contains exactly one fox card and one bird card, neither an ambush nor a dominance card. Compare adding just one of them to Build and retaining the other.

| Decree column | Existing cards |
| --- | --- |
| Recruit | Bird Loyal Vizier, fox card |
| Move | Bird Loyal Vizier, rabbit card |
| Battle | Empty |
| Build | Empty; add fox or bird now |

The Eyrie has only two roosts on the map. Fox clearing D contains two Eyrie warriors and a roost. Adjacent rabbit clearing E also contains two Eyrie warriors and a roost. E connects to mouse clearing F, which is empty and has an open slot. The Eyrie has 16 warriors in supply and 10 points. There are no enemies, ruins or other effects in these three clearings. No other fox clearing is ruled by the Eyrie, and the omitted map supplies no extra action targets.

Neither path crafts cards. Both resolve the first two columns identically: recruit twice at D, increasing its warriors from two to four; use the bird move to move one from D to E, then the rabbit move to move one from E to F. D now has three, E two and F one, with fourteen in supply. The Eyrie rules all three. Battle is empty, so proceed to Build.

The bird path can put a third roost at F. The fox path cannot build at mouse clearing F, while D, the only ruled fox clearing, already has a roost. The action cannot be fulfilled, causing immediate turmoil. See [Law 7.4–7.8, pages 6–7](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0106/0162/7706/files/Root_Base_Law_Oct_2025.pdf?v=1774903756).

| Card added | Build and aftermath | Turmoil loss | Evening roost points | Total after the scoring step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bird | Build at F; retain Builder and Decree | 0 | Three roosts score 2 | 12 |
| Fox | Cannot build; purge ordinary Decree cards and change leader | 2 | Two roosts score 1 | 9 |

The fox path loses 2 points for the two bird Viziers; the fox card adds no penalty. Assume the Despot is available: select it and reassign the Viziers to Move and Build. Discard the ordinary Recruit fox, Move rabbit and newly added Build fox. Earlier recruits and moves remain; Evening scoring still happens. This passage stops after that scoring step, before drawing cards. Track values are visible in [Learning to Play, page 12](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0106/0162/7706/files/Root_Base_Learn_to_Play_web_Oct_15_2020.pdf?v=1603389572).

The bird preserves three more points now and keeps a longer Decree. But it is not spent after use: another build remains compulsory on future turns, and this bird would add to the penalty in a later turmoil. The retained hand card also differs—fox in one path, bird in the other—so present points are not the whole comparison.

The design question changes from “What does one more action provide?” to “When does an added action become a promise that must be fulfilled?” An opponent’s Decree is not an ordinary action menu. The Eyrie may have to move or build when the Marquise could simply stop. We have not simulated how opponents might block the next turn, so this is not a claim that the bird is always the better long-term choice.

## Angle three: Removing soldiers need not remove influence

The third passage begins with a battle; any earlier movement has already ended. A fox clearing contains three cats, one Alliance warrior and one sympathy token, with no buildings. The Marquise has 8 points and two hand cards, one fox and one bird. The Alliance has two supporters and no bases anywhere on the map. Neither side uses an ambush or another battle effect; the Marquise declines Field Hospitals. Fix the dice at 3 and 1.

Compare the official rule with a site-created hypothetical change: remove only the Alliance’s Guerrilla War reversal of defensive dice. Everything else stays fixed. Rolled hits still cannot exceed that side’s warrior count, and warriors are removed before buildings or tokens. See [Law 4.3, page 4, and 8.2.2 and 8.2.6, page 7](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0106/0162/7706/files/Root_Base_Law_Oct_2025.pdf?v=1774903756).

| Resolution | Official rule | Hypothetical removal of defensive reversal only |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marquise hits | Low die 1: one hit | High die 3: three hits |
| Alliance hits | High die 3, capped by one warrior: one hit | Low die 1: one hit |
| Cats / Alliance warriors afterward | 2 / 0 | 2 / 0 |
| Sympathy | Remains | Removed; one excess hit has no target |
| Marquise points | 8 | 9 |
| Marquise hand size / Alliance supporters | 2 / 2 | 1 / 3 |

Removing sympathy in the hypothetical path triggers Outrage. The Marquise gives the fox card to the supporters stack and retains the bird. Both paths lose exactly one warrior per side, but leave different influence on the map and different card resources. The official path removes no sympathy and includes no movement into the clearing during this passage, so neither triggers Outrage here. Each path ends after the battle and its triggered effects resolve.

In this example, defensive reversal mainly protects the token behind the warrior. It does not let a lone Alliance warrior inflict three hits. If another battle begins, the official path now has no Alliance warriors there, so the conditions change again. “Strong defence” needs an object and a time horizon: what survives, and for how long? Casualty counts alone miss this effect.

The publisher’s [Hard Cuts development diary](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-hard-cuts-the-continued-development-of-root) records an Alliance redesign: after removing the old conspiracies system, staging a Marquise–Eyrie conflict helped express rebellion through sympathy and base pieces on the map. Our resulting design question is: where should a thematic relationship produce an observable change of state? This 2018 account describes a development draft. It does not replace the 2025 rules used above or establish that removing defensive reversal improves the game.

## Self-checks with answers

1. Why does the Eyrie rule B in the first example? One cat plus one building ties two Eyrie warriors, and the Eyrie wins ties for rule. Wood adds nothing to that count.
2. Must the Marquise destroy the enemy at B to open access to wood? Not here. Adding a warrior establishes a strict majority without a battle.
3. Does turmoil undo the second example’s recruits and moves? No. D retains three warriors, E two and F one. The purge, leader change and end of Daylight do not rewind them.
4. Why does the fox path end on 9 rather than 8 points? Start at 10, lose two for the Viziers, then score one for two roosts in Evening.
5. Why does the Alliance’s high die of 3 cause only one hit in the third example? It has one warrior. Reversing the dice does not remove the warrior cap on rolled hits.
6. Why give the Alliance a card under the hypothetical change? Only defensive dice reversal was removed. Outrage for removing sympathy still applies.

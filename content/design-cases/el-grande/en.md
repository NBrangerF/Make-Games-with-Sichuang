# El Grande: Why Do Order and Destination Change the Value of the Same People?

You have the most knights in a region and appear to lead. Then the King moves, changing where deployment is allowed, or the castle opens and more knights arrive. El Grande’s map needs recounting, and the contest for a chance to change those numbers begins before deployment.

This article uses the four-player base game in Hans im Glück’s 2023 English rules, on the four-to-five-player side of the board. It excludes the mini expansions, alternative cards, two-player dummy and short game. The [publisher’s page](https://www.hans-im-glueck.de/en/game/el-grande-2/) identifies Wolfgang Kramer and Richard Ulrich as the designers. Our situations and comparisons are original analysis, not their test records or a strategy guide to an entire game.

Caballeros are the knights counted for majorities. The Court is a player’s personal supply; the Province holds those not yet recruited. Castillo means the physical castle. Castilla is a region on the map: they are different places.

## Angle one: Earlier access can come with less supply in hand

Consider Lin’s first round in a four-player game. Each player has seven Caballeros in their Court, twenty-one in the Province and two in their starting region. Jo, Mei and Bo have publicly played Power cards 11, 8 and 4, in that order. Lin plays last and can choose 13 or 9 without duplicating an earlier value. The five Action cards are already face up, including Your Majesty, which permits five placements, and Fiesta, which permits three.

We track Lin’s supply and castle contributions. Whoever chooses Your Majesty declines to move the King. If Lin selects Fiesta, Lin declines its special scoring. The others’ subsequent actions do not change Lin’s Caballeros. Compare these specified sequences:

| Lin’s card | Order and Action card | Recruitment | Deployment | After Lin’s turn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 13 | Lin acts first and takes Your Majesty | None from the Province | Five into the Castillo | Two remain in Court; five contributed this round |
| 9 | Jo takes Your Majesty first; Lin then takes Fiesta | Two from the Province, making nine in Court | Three into the Castillo | Six remain in Court; three contributed this round |

The two recruits on card 9, zero on cards 12 and 13, and the separation between Court supply and deployment are grounded in [rules pages 4–6](https://www.hans-im-glueck.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/ElGrande_2023_rule_EN_20-07-23-komprimiert.pdf). A Power value of nine does not mean nine recruits. A placement allowance does not create the people to fill it.

With 13, Lin has contributed more to the castle but retains four fewer people for later deployment. That difference combines two forgone recruits with two additional placements. The large number on the Power card therefore connects three separate things: timing, available actions and personal supply. It is not a single measure of strength.

Nor does this prove that 9 is always more efficient. We specified Jo’s choice. If Jo takes another action, acting later might not lose Lin access to Your Majesty. The castle situation, other available actions and future rounds could all change what Lin wants. For a design of your own, separate the price of earlier access into its parts: current supply, future card options and the action forgone now. Calling the largest number “the strong card” hides that work.

## Angle two: Two actions on one card can change each other’s legal targets

Start a different four-player game. The King is in Castilla. Lin’s Grande and two Caballeros are in Galicia, and Lin has seven in Court. Jo, Mei and Bo start in Navarra, Toledo and Sevilla respectively. Lin plays 13 and chooses Your Majesty first. We examine this one turn before anybody else acts.

Lin can deploy up to five Caballeros and move the King. Castilla and Galicia are adjacent, but the King’s own region cannot receive Caballeros. Here are two complete orders:

| Order | First action | Second action | Lin’s resulting position |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deploy, then move the King | Place five from Court in Galicia | Move the King to Galicia | Seven in Galicia, none in Castilla; two in Court |
| Move the King, then deploy | Move the King to Galicia | Place five from Court in adjacent Castilla | Two in Galicia, five in Castilla; two in Court |

Both branches end with the King in Galicia and the same five people deployed. In the first, reinforcement happens before Galicia becomes protected from changes. In the second, moving the King changes the legal deployment targets, so those five cannot enter Galicia. [Rules pages 5–6; Your Majesty on page 12](https://www.hans-im-glueck.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/ElGrande_2023_rule_EN_20-07-23-komprimiert.pdf)

Lin cannot place two, move the King and then place three: each action must be completed before the other begins. Moving the King is also a different operation from moving Caballeros between regions. Their restrictions cannot be interchanged.

The difference changes the options available when the others take their turns. Seven in Galicia do not score immediately or grant permanent ownership. The King can move later, and a later scoring considers the position then. A lesson about area competition that shows only final colors and quantities misses another question: can this place currently be changed?

For your own rules, choose two actions and work through both orders. Compare more than the sum of their rewards. Does an intermediate step change eligibility, the kind of object that can be targeted, or the set of possible destinations? Sequence can create a decision without adding another resource.

## Angle three: How many are in the castle and where they will go are different uncertainties

Now consider an independent position at the end of round three, just before the first general scoring. The King is in Granada. Lin, Jo, Mei and Bo have their Grandes in Toledo, Aragón, Cataluña and Sevilla respectively. Galicia and Navarra have no Grandes or scoring tiles. Announced contributions to the castle total two for Lin, two for Jo, one for Mei and none for Bo. We give the reader these totals for calculation; players cannot open the castle simply to check their memory.

The relevant positions are below. The other regions are unaffected by these destination choices. Our subtotal covers the castle and these two regions at this scoring, not a complete game score.

| Before scoring | Lin | Jo | Mei | Bo | Base points for first / second / third |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Castillo | 2 | 2 | 1 | 0 | 5 / 3 / 1 |
| Galicia | 1 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 4 / 2 / 0 |
| Navarra | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 5 / 3 / 1 |

Players commit to destinations before the castle opens. Its contents score, then move as each player’s single group to the chosen region, before the regions score. Lin therefore cannot wait to see Jo’s destination and then revise the choice. [Rules pages 6–8](https://www.hans-im-glueck.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/ElGrande_2023_rule_EN_20-07-23-komprimiert.pdf)

Lin sends two to Galicia and Mei sends one to Navarra. Bo has no castle Caballeros; set Bo’s disc to Toledo for this example. Change only Jo’s destination:

| Jo chooses | Galicia after movement and its points | Navarra after movement and its points | Subtotal including the castle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Navarra | Lin 3, Jo 2: Lin scores 4, Jo 2 | Lin 1, Jo 3, Mei 2: Lin scores 1, Jo 5, Mei 3 | Lin 8, Jo 10, Mei 4, Bo 0 |
| Galicia | Lin 3, Jo 4: Lin scores 2, Jo 4 | Lin 1, Jo 1, Mei 2: Lin scores 1, Jo 1, Mei 5 | Lin 6, Jo 8, Mei 6, Bo 0 |

Castle points are unchanged: Lin and Jo tie for the largest group and receive the second award, three each; Mei receives the third award, one. In the second branch’s Navarra, Lin and Jo tie behind Mei, so each receives the third award, one. An absent color earns nothing merely because a rank is unoccupied. Neither destination offers a King or Grande bonus here. [Ties and bonuses, page 8](https://www.hans-im-glueck.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/ElGrande_2023_rule_EN_20-07-23-komprimiert.pdf)

Jo’s switch takes the lead in Galicia away from Lin, yet lowers Jo’s subtotal from ten to eight. Mei changes no decision and rises from four to six. In multiplayer majority systems, effects can travel between places and players. A local victory is not a substitute for comparing the connected settlement. This still does not establish Jo’s best move for the whole game: future positioning and other scores have not been reduced to this little table.

The castle combines memory of publicly announced inputs with uncertainty about a newly hidden decision. Even perfect recall of the colors inside cannot reveal Jo’s disc. When designing a hidden-information component, decide separately what history is recoverable, what choice is still undisclosed and when commitment occurs. A transparent container might reduce memory demands without removing the destination decision. That is our proposed design experiment, not an official variant or an experiment the designers have reported.

## Designer’s account: What survives a change of theme?

In a [2015 Brettspielbox interview](https://brettspielbox.de/interview-mit-wolfgang-kramer-teil-2/), Kramer discusses finding a new theme after a competing Trojan Horse game appeared. He says that his and Ulrich’s mechanisms remained distinct, giving them a basis for further development with a Spanish setting. The author’s anecdote reproduced in the interview explicitly says both theme and system changed.

This supports a particular development history, not a claim that renaming components completes a retheme. It does not explain how our two King sequences were invented or provide results from testing a transparent castle. Keeping the record separate from our analysis lets us ask a useful further question: which roles, actions and relationships in a new setting help explain the rules, and which still need explicit explanation? Historical associations do not themselves grant permission to take an action.

## Check your understanding, with answers

1. After recruiting two with card 9, can Lin deploy additional people straight from the Province? No. The Action card gives a ceiling; the available people still come from the Court.
2. Why do the King examples differ when both end with five deployed and the King in Galicia? The moment of the King’s move changes legal deployment targets. The two actions cannot be interleaved.
3. Does remembering every color’s castle count determine the regional scores? No. Destinations remain secret, and those groups arrive before regional scoring.
4. Does Jo’s lead in Galicia increase Jo’s three-location subtotal? No. It falls from ten to eight once the loss in Navarra is included.

# Azul: Why Do Tiles Taken Not Equal Points Earned This Round?

A single tile might complete a pattern line, connect two sections of a wall, or fall to the floor. Three independent situations examine how shared supplies, staged resolution and the timing of the ending change a choice.

This article uses Michael Kiesling’s original *Azul*, with both players using the colored wall. Our fixed reference is the six-page English base rulebook, bearing ©2018 Plan B Games, linked by the [publisher](https://www.nextmove-games.com/en/azul/azul-game/). Summer Pavilion, Stained Glass, Mini, expansions and the gray-wall variant are outside this article. The situations and design analysis are ours, not the designer’s playtest records.

Every example has two players, Lin and Jo, alternating turns. A “pattern line” is on the left, with capacities one through five. Wall rows run top to bottom and columns left to right. B, Y, R, K and W mean blue, yellow, red, black and white; white identifies the patterned tile with a white background. The colored wall’s five rows are BYRKW, WBYRK, KWBYR, RKWBY and YRKWB. [Components and board: rules page 2](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-nextmove/uploads/sites/4/2024/06/EN-Azul-Rules-Next-Move-web.pdf).

## Angle one: What you leave becomes somebody else’s next set of options

It is round one. Both players have zero points and empty walls, pattern lines and floors. Lin starts, placing the starting player marker in the center. The five factories contain all of these tiles: F1 = BBRR, F2 = BBYY, F3 = KKKK, F4 = WWWW and F5 = YYYY. There are no colored tiles in the center, eighty remain in the bag, and the box lid is empty.

Lin takes both blues from F1 into pattern line 2, pushing both reds into the center. Jo takes both blues from F2 into line 2 and pushes both yellows into the center. The center now holds two reds, two yellows and the marker. Compare two specified sequences of responses:

| Player | A: Lin takes the central reds now | B: Lin takes white first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lin | Both central reds: one in line 1, one on the floor; also take the marker | F4’s four whites into line 4 |
| Jo | F3’s four blacks into line 4 | Both central reds: one in line 1, one on the floor; also take the marker |
| Lin | F4’s four whites into line 4 | F3’s four blacks into line 5 |
| Jo | F5’s four yellows into line 5 | F5’s four yellows into line 4 |
| Lin | Both central yellows into line 3 | Both central yellows into line 3 |

All factories and the center are now empty. In A, Lin moves a red, a blue and a white onto the wall. None is directly adjacent to another, so each scores one. The marker and red tile on the floor cost 1 + 1: Lin has 3 − 2 = 1 point. Jo’s blue and black score one each, totaling two. In B, Lin’s blue and white total two; Jo’s red, blue and yellow total three, reduced by two for the marker and red tile, leaving one. Neither branch ends the game. [Taking tiles, overflow and resolution: rules pages 3–5](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-nextmove/uploads/sites/4/2024/06/EN-Azul-Rules-Next-Move-web.pdf).

A retains Lin’s two yellows in line 3 and Jo’s four yellows in line 5. B retains Lin’s two yellows in line 3 and four blacks in line 5; Jo has no incomplete lines. Each completed line sends just one tile to the wall; its other tiles and fallen colored tiles go into the lid. Lin starts next round in A, Jo in B. Both branches refill five factories from the eighty tiles still in the bag, without immediately recycling the lid to obtain preferred colors.

Lin cannot take just one central red or split those two reds between pattern lines 1 and 3. A choice therefore changes personal capacity, the shared supply and the next starting player together. Jo’s responses are specified, so the scores are not a forced outcome. They demonstrate that waiting can let somebody else take the group first. The shared center does not reserve supplies for you.

## Angle two: Completing one line can also increase a later tile’s score

Use a different, later-game snapshot. All five factories are empty, the center holds only one black, and Lin acts next. Lin has three points. The only wall tiles are white at (2,1), yellow at (2,3) and white at (3,2). Pattern lines contain, respectively, one yellow, two blues, two blacks, four reds, and nothing in line 5. Lin’s floor holds only the starting player marker. Jo has zero points, an empty wall, four reds in line 5, no other pattern tiles, and six yellows occupying the first six floor spaces.

This example and the next specify their supplies: four tiles of each color remain in the bag; every colored tile not listed on the table or in the bag is in the box lid. There are no other table tiles. These snapshots do not reconstruct the draws in all earlier rounds. Every unlisted wall space is empty.

Lin takes the last black and compares two legal destinations. Complete pattern line 3, then resolve from top to bottom:

| Tile moved to the wall | Connected tiles at that moment | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yellow at row 1, column 2 | No directly adjacent tile yet | 1 |
| Blue at row 2, column 2 | White–blue–yellow horizontally; yellow–blue–white vertically | 3 + 3 = 6 |
| Black at row 3, column 1 | Black–white horizontally; white–black vertically | 2 + 2 = 4 |
| Red at row 4, column 1 | White–black–red vertically | 3 |

Lin reaches 3 + 1 + 6 + 4 + 3 − 1 = 16 points, with every pattern line cleared. Alternatively, Lin deliberately puts the last black on the floor. Line 3 still has only two blacks and contributes no wall tile. The other three placements score one, six and one. A gap above the new red prevents it connecting to the white in row 2. Lin reaches 3 + 1 + 6 + 1 − 2 = 9 and retains the two blacks in line 3. In both branches Jo retains four reds; the eleven-point floor penalty cannot take Jo below zero. Nobody ends the game; Lin retrieves the marker and prepares the next round. [Retention, order and horizontal/vertical scoring: rules pages 4–5](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-nextmove/uploads/sites/4/2024/06/EN-Azul-Rules-Next-Move-web.pdf).

The seven-point difference combines four from the black placement, two extra from the later red, and one avoided floor penalty. When both directions connect, the new tile belongs to both counted lines; diagonal contact does not count. Resolving line 2 before returning to line 1 would omit the newly placed yellow and is not a permitted change of order.

This does not make longer pattern lines universally better. The effect requires these existing neighbors and an already completed fourth line. In a system that accumulates resources before converting them, examine both the completion requirement and the conversion order. Counting collected pieces alone misses how their values depend on one another.

## Angle three: The ending determines when completed goals pay out

Use another position, with Lin on twenty-six points and Jo on six. All five factories are empty. The center contains one white, and Lin acts next. The table shows every tile on both walls. A dot is an empty space, not a free choice of color.

| Wall row | Lin | Jo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | BYRK· | B···· |
| 2 | W···K | ·B··· |
| 3 | ·W··R | ··B·· |
| 4 | ··W·Y | ···B· |
| 5 | ···WB | ···W· |

Lin’s line 1 is empty. Lines 2 through 5 contain one blue, two yellows, three blacks and four reds respectively; the floor is empty. Jo has one yellow in line 2, three reds in line 4 and five blues in line 5, no other pattern tiles, and only the marker on the floor. After taking the last white, Lin can complete line 1 or let the white fall to the floor. Lines 2 through 5 cannot receive it: each corresponding wall row already contains a white.

Complete line 1 and place white at (1,5). It joins five tiles horizontally and five vertically, scoring ten immediately. Lin has completed a horizontal row, but Jo still moves a blue from line 5 to (5,5), scoring two with the white to its left, then loses one for the marker. The scores reach thirty-six and seven. Only after the entire wall-tiling phase do end bonuses apply. Lin’s completed row, completed column and five whites add 2 + 7 + 10 = 19, producing a final score of fifty-five. Jo’s five blues add ten, producing seventeen. The game ends and factories are not refilled. [Ending and bonuses: rules page 6](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-nextmove/uploads/sites/4/2024/06/EN-Azul-Rules-Next-Move-web.pdf).

If Lin puts the white on the floor, Lin adds no wall tile and drops to twenty-five after the one-point penalty. Jo still reaches seven. Jo now has five blues, but neither player has completed a horizontal row: the ten-point bonus is not awarded yet, and play continues. Jo holds the marker and refills five four-tile factories from the twenty tiles in the bag. The random groups are unspecified and cannot be credited with future points. The last white returns to the lid in this branch. Both branches retain the incomplete pattern lines; the ending branch offers no further opportunity to complete them.

We are comparing states after the present resolution. Fifty-five versus twenty-five does not prove that the first branch must be better over the whole game: the second still has future actions. The situation separates two conditions. A completed horizontal row triggers the ending; rows, columns and complete colors determine end bonuses. The player who triggers the ending cannot cancel another player’s wall placements this round. When designing goals, write “award points” and “stop further play” separately to see what players actually control.

## Designer’s account: The destination of leftover tiles changed

In the [Brettspielbox interview dated July 31, 2018](https://brettspielbox.de/brettspielbox-interview-michael-kiesling/), Kiesling recalls a publisher returning the game with feedback. Tiles originally passed clockwise; he changed this so they went into the center. He describes the revision while discussing the importance of accepting criticism from others.

This supports an actual change to the passing relationship. It does not make our seven-turn sequence the designer’s experiment, or quantify how much enjoyment the revision added. Our inference from angle one is that changing where leftovers go also changes who can select them on a subsequent turn. Comparing the old and new mechanisms further would require the prototype’s actual details and playtesting.

## Check your understanding, with answers

1. In angle one, can Lin place the second red in another pattern line after filling line 1? No. That selection can enter one pattern line; overflow goes to the floor.
2. Why does angle two’s blue score six rather than five? The horizontal three and vertical three are counted separately, each including the new tile at their intersection.
3. If the last black falls to the floor in angle two, are the two blacks in line 3 removed? No. The incomplete line remains; the resulting gap also leaves the red scoring only one.
4. Can Jo still move a blue from the five-blue pattern line after Lin completes a horizontal row? Yes. Finish the entire wall-tiling phase before awarding end bonuses.
5. Does a completed column or five tiles of one color end the game? No. Jo’s five blues in the continuing branch do not immediately add ten points either.
6. What do three occupied floor spaces cost? 1 + 1 + 2 = 4. A score of two after wall placements drops to zero, not minus two. If all seven floor spaces are occupied, further fallen colored tiles go into the lid without adding penalty spaces. [Rules pages 4–5](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-nextmove/uploads/sites/4/2024/06/EN-Azul-Rules-Next-Move-web.pdf).

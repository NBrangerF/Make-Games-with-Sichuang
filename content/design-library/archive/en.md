# The archive: two records kept, but different questions answered

The same number of retained records can leave different questions answerable. This develops the setting of an archive that forgets, using an explicit dependency before discussing preservation choices. All three records are fictional, without real people, institutions, or historical events.

## Three records and two storage places

One archivist sees three public cards. A records an artwork. B links A to its maker's name. C records an independent event. Keep exactly two cards in the archive through one selection; the third leaves the queryable collection. No later swapping or recalling the missing card from memory is allowed.

Then process two fixed queries. Q1 asks for A's maker and requires both A and B. Q2 asks about C's event and requires C alone. Queries and evidence requirements are public before selection, not surprise questions revealed afterward.

If requirements are unmet, record “cannot answer” without guessing or extra penalty. Each answered query scores 1. End after both, recording the answered set as well as the total. Equal totals tie, without an unstated priority. There are no costs, randomness, other players, or extra storage methods.

Requiring both A and B is the model's chosen evidence chain: a card containing a name alone does not establish a queryable link to the work.

## Keep A and C: objects without the connecting record

Select A and C, removing B. The artwork record remains, but Q1's maker link does not, so Q1 cannot be answered. C supports Q2.

Result: **1 point, answered set {Q2}**. Preserving two records does not preserve every relationship involving the artwork. The gap concerns a connection, beyond a count of objects.

## Keep A and B: preserve the link, lose the other event

Reset and retain A and B. Their complete chain supports Q1, while missing C prevents answering Q2.

| Retained | Q1: artwork maker | Q2: independent event | Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| A, C | Missing link; cannot answer | Answerable | 1 |
| A, B | Answerable | Missing record; cannot answer | 1 |

Again, **1 point**. Equal totals do not preserve identical knowledge or automatically make one selection better. Each supports a different question. Prioritizing one query would require an explicit value choice, not an unannounced scoring change afterward.

## Preserving records also shapes what can be seen later

The archivist selects what remains under a two-place limit. Relationships among records determine answerability. Two individually informative cards do not necessarily form a complete evidence chain.

This differs from scoring simply for collecting two cards. It also differs from questioning in a deduction puzzle: queries are known beforehand, and the player changes the available evidence rather than revealing an unknown answer. Reclassification or later acquisition would need rules explaining their effects on subsequent queries.

## Check and model boundary

Can keeping B and C answer Q1?

**No.** B provides the maker link, but A is outside the queryable collection, so the pair requirement fails. Q2 remains answerable: 1 point and set {Q2}. A,C and B,C therefore answer the same two-query subset here; that does not make them equivalent for every possible future question.

This is not an archival policy. Authorization, privacy, disputed evidence, and preservation obligations are absent. “Cannot answer” means the rules do not support a response, not that the historical event never happened. Observe whether readers can identify each answer's supporting records and missing connections before expanding the collection.

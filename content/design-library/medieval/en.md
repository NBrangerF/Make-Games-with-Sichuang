# Medieval themes: goods in hand, but who may enter the market?

A period setting can shape land, status, organizations, and permissions, beyond providing castle artwork. This original episode selects the relationship of who may sell: control of one market pass changes another player's legal choices. The market is fictional, not a reconstruction of any place or century.

## Two partners and one pass

A holds the pass; B is a craftsperson without it. They cooperate. Each starts with 1 cloth and 2 coins, with the only market pass held by A. Everything is public and deterministic. Play two rounds, each giving A one action, followed by B one action.

Choose one option: if holding the pass and a cloth, sell your own cloth for 3 coins from the bank; transfer the pass you hold to the other player; or wait. Selling removes the cloth permanently. The bank can fund every sale. Transfer is free and immediate but uses an action, which cannot also sell.

There is no buying a pass, substituting money for permission, selling remotely for another player, or obtaining more cloth. Reselect an unavailable action without spending it. After both rounds, total their coins: at least 10 means shared success, otherwise shared failure. Wealth does not decide an individual winner. Shared outcomes do not give both players identical permissions.

## Keep the pass throughout

In round one, A sells, increasing from 2 to 5 coins. B has cloth but no pass and waits in this sequence. In round two, A retains the pass and waits; B waits again.

Final coins are A 5 and B 2: **7 total, shared failure**. Neither goods nor a buyer are missing. Selling permission is the constraint, and A's money does not automatically authorize B to act.

## Transfer permission after selling

Reset. Round one again has A sell and B wait. In round two A spends the action transferring the pass; B then sells, increasing from 2 to 5. Each ends with 5: **10 total, shared success**.

| Sequence | A in round two | B in round two | Final coins A / B | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retain pass | Wait | Wait | 5 / 2 | 7, failure |
| Transfer pass | Transfer | Sell | 5 / 5 | 10, success |

Transfer produces neither cloth nor coins directly. It changes the next player's legal choices. That is more specific than whether the component is called a magical card or a market document.

## How does the setting distribute authority?

The roles are unequal at the start: A controls access; B cannot unilaterally grant permission. Limited actions also give transfer a timing cost. Remove only the pass requirement for selling, and both can sell in round one, then wait to succeed at the end of round two.

This is a power relationship chosen by rules, not a general account of historical markets. Another theme might make the pass a tool, license, or communication permission. Retheming requires reconsidering whether the restriction still expresses the intended relationship.

## Simplifications and the next observation

The partners cooperate, income is public, and only the total matters. There are no status changes, variable prices, residents' needs, or legal procedures. One transfer resolves this episode's restriction; that does not establish how real institutions can be changed.

Observe whether readers confuse having enough resources with being permitted to act. Then consider which role could change the rules themselves. Individual competition would require new winning, trading, and refusal consequences; the same shared-success explanation would no longer apply.

## Check: can B buy eligibility for 2 coins?

A retains the pass. B has 2 coins and 1 cloth. Can B pay the bank to become eligible, then sell?

**No.** Buying permission is not an action in these rules. B can wait; only the holder can transfer the pass. Adding a purchase option would require a price, a decision about pass exclusivity, and whether selling uses a separate action.

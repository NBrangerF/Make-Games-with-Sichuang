# Environmental themes: what must remain for recovery to continue?

An action can provide an immediate harvest while changing what remains possible next round. An environmental theme can begin with that relationship over time: are players managing goods, or a shared condition that keeps changing? This original resource-pool model uses invented quantities and recovery rules.

## Complete rules for two harvest rounds

A and B jointly manage a pool containing 6 resources, its maximum. Their shared store starts empty. Everything is public, with no randomness. There are two rounds. In each, A acts once, then B once. Take 0, 1, or 2 resources from the pool into the store, without exceeding what remains. Taking 0 means waiting. There are no other costs, and stored resources cannot be returned.

After both actions each round, check the pool. With at least 3 remaining, add 2, up to the maximum of 6. With fewer than 3, add nothing. Recovery never occurs between players' actions. Apply this step after round two as well, then end.

Shared success requires both at least 5 stored and at least 3 in the pool. Otherwise both fail. Extra stored goods cannot compensate for an insufficient pool. There are no individual points or tie-breaks.

## Take as much as possible first

In round one, A takes 2, leaving 4; B takes 2, leaving 2. The store holds 4, but the pool is below 3, so nothing returns.

In round two, A takes 1, leaving 1; B takes 1, leaving 0. The store now holds 6. Again there is no recovery. **Store 6, pool 0: shared failure.** Both rounds yielded goods, but the condition for replenishment disappeared.

## Take one fewer and compare the full outcome

Reset to pool 6 and store 0, retaining every rule.

| Round | Taken by A / B | Pool before recovery | Pool after recovery | Store total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One | 2 / 1 | 3 | 5 | 3 |
| Two | 1 / 1 | 3 | 5 | 5 |

The final state is **store 5, pool 5: shared success**. Compared with the first sequence, players collect one fewer resource but retain five more. Meeting the recovery condition twice creates the difference; the rules do not award points for saying that conservation matters.

Taking 2 / 0 in round two produces the same totals. With public information and shared results, this model does not distinguish which person takes less. It does not establish how real responsibilities should be distributed.

## Where does the theme enter the rules?

Players are joint managers. Their action is harvesting; the pressure connects present needs to future availability. The crucial rule makes current stock determine subsequent replenishment. Remove recovery and only 6 resources exist across both rounds: storing at least 5 while retaining at least 3 becomes impossible.

Private benefits and shared losses would be another design, requiring ownership, winning conditions, and communication rules. There is no secret beneficiary here. This example cannot establish that competitors will voluntarily take less or serve as a universal model of common-resource dilemmas.

## What was simplified?

There are no species, pollution, geography, or real growth rates. “Keep 3 to add 2” is a deliberately chosen threshold, not an ecological law. Resources are identical; recovery has neither delay nor uncertainty. A game about an actual environment would need research into the particular relationship it represents.

Ask readers to predict whether taking one fewer changes the next replenishment. That creates a more specific question than whether they like green artwork. Actual observation would still be needed to evaluate understanding; this worked example checks rule outcomes only.

## Check: after taking exactly 3 in round one

After recovery, the pool holds 5 and the store 3. What if both players take 2 in round two?

**Failure.** Only 1 remains before recovery, so nothing is added. The final store holds 7 and the pool 1. Restraint in the first round does not make every later choice sustainable.

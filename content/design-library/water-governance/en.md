# Shared water: can last season's allocation cap meet this season's needs?

Upstream withdrawals leave the remainder for downstream users. One cap can produce different results as supply and needs change. This original two-season episode develops the shared-water setting by comparing fixed and revised enforceable caps, without real water-rights or policy conclusions.

## Three positions and two seasons

Upstream U, middle M, and downstream D take water in that order each season. Supply and needs are public beforehand:

| Season | Supply | U needs | M needs | D needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One | 6 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Two | 4 | 1 | 2 | 1 |

Each chooses a nonnegative integer, no greater than the remaining water or current cap. Taking 0 is legal. There is no borrowing, trading, or external supply. The remainder passes downstream. Unclaimed water leaves at season end; personal surplus cannot be carried between seasons. There are no fees or randomness.

Check each person's needs separately each season. Meeting all needs in both seasons means shared success; otherwise failure, with no total-withdrawal ranking. Under the fixed system everyone has cap 2 in both seasons. The revised system retains that first season, then announces caps 1, 2, 1 for U, M, D before season-two withdrawals. These caps restrict legal actions, rather than offering optional advice.

Both worked sequences specify everyone taking the maximum currently permitted. This checks consequences, not a claim about how real participants necessarily behave.

## Fixed cap: upstream acts legally, downstream still lacks water

In season one, U takes 2, leaving 4; M takes 2, leaving 2; D takes 2. All needs are met.

In season two, U legally takes 2, leaving 2. M takes 2, leaving 0. D gets 0 instead of the required 1. Allocation **2, 2, 0 fails**. U needs only 1 but remains entitled by these rules to take 2. Individually legal choices do not guarantee jointly satisfied needs.

## Revised cap: put the change into legal choices

Season one is unchanged. In season two U's cap is 1, leaving 3 after withdrawal. M takes 2, leaving 1; D takes 1. Allocation **1, 2, 1** satisfies both seasons and succeeds.

| Season-two system | U gets | M gets | D gets | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed cap 2 | 2 | 2 | 0 | D short |
| Revised caps 1, 2, 1 | 1 | 2 | 1 | All met |

Supply and needs did not change between comparisons; permitted withdrawals did. This works for the specified maximum-taking sequence, not every legal choice: taking 0 can still leave someone's need unmet.

## Distinguish negotiation, promises, and enforcement

If U merely promises to take 1 while the legal cap remains 2, taking 2 is still legal. A promise has not automatically become an enforced rule. Consequences for breaking it would require explicit records, responsibilities, and repair procedures, not a retroactive denial of an allowed choice.

Location and order transmit consequences through the same finite supply. There is no natural recovery or queued environmental load here. Unlike the related environmental examples, this episode concerns allocation rules changing with conditions.

## Check and model boundary

In season two U already took 2, leaving 2. M needs 2 and D needs 1. Can a cleverer split between the last two meet both needs?

**No.** They need 3 in total with only 2 left, a shortage of 1. Without external supply or stored water, changing allocation order cannot create it.

Quantities, needs, order, and caps are fictional, not actual water rights, fairness standards, or historical relationships. Observe whether readers distinguish permission, keeping promises, and eventual sufficiency without treating the episode as a governance policy.

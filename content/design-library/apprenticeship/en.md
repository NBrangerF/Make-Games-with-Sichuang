# Apprenticeship: the products exist, but who practiced their own task?

If the goal is learning a technique, counting finished objects may hide who received practice opportunities. Two apprentices each begin with one skill. This original comparison examines making things for each other before teaching versus demonstrating first. A skill marker is a game permission, not evidence that real learning happens instantly.

## Four materials and two learners

A starts knowing cutting; B knows joining. The goal is for A personally to make A's joining project and B personally to make B's cutting project. Four materials are shared; neither project exists. Play two rounds, A then B taking one action each, for four total. All states are public, without random quality outcomes.

An action may spend 1 material to demonstrate a known skill to the other player, immediately and permanently giving that skill to the learner; spend 1 material to use a known skill to make either player's target project, recording its maker; or wait. Demonstrating and making each use one action and cannot be combined. A teacher retains the demonstrated skill.

Making the other's project is legal, with accurate authorship. Remaking a project replaces its maker record without refunding materials. Without the material or skill, reselect legally without spending the action. There are no extra skills, replenishment, or automatic completions.

After action four, shared success requires both projects to exist and each to have been made by its intended learner. Possessing someone else's work does not count as personal practice. Appearance and prices do not score.

## Make each other's projects first

A cuts B's project; B joins A's, using two materials. In round two A demonstrates cutting to B and B demonstrates joining to A, using the remaining two.

Both objects exist and both players now know both skills, but each recorded maker is the other player. **Personal-practice completions: 0, failure.** Learning afterward cannot retroactively change who made an earlier object.

## Demonstrate first, then preserve time to practice

Reset. Transfer skills before personal making:

| Action | Choice | Materials left | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A demonstrates cutting to B | 3 | B also knows cutting |
| 2 | B demonstrates joining to A | 2 | A also knows joining |
| 3 | A makes A's joining project | 1 | A completes personal practice |
| 4 | B makes B's cutting project | 0 | B completes personal practice |

The same four materials and actions produce **2 personal-practice completions, success**. Ability and practice opportunities arrive in the needed order, without an extra reward for this sequence.

## The goal determines what must be recorded

These apprentices have strengths and learning needs. Demonstration changes another person's available capability; making changes an object and its authorship record. Recording only whether objects exist would erase the distinction, so this goal requires the maker field.

This does not make doing work for another person universally bad. If the goal changed to delivering two objects promptly, the first two proxy actions would already meet that different target. Learning and delivery goals can coexist, but one cannot silently substitute for the other.

## Check and model boundary

After demonstrating cutting to B, can A still cut?

**Yes.** A capability is shared, not a unique skill card taken away. Treating demonstration as transferring an exclusive tool would alter later legal actions.

Instant binary skills, uniform material costs, and error-free making are deliberate simplifications, not claims about real learning after one demonstration. Observe whether readers separately record knowing, possessing, and personally making, and whether they reserve an opportunity for practice.

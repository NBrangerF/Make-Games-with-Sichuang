# A neighborhood festival: the shows are over, but who handles the shared wrap-up?

A shared event has visible performances and work that makes them possible. Here two organizers each bring one show, and the same four opportunities must accommodate promotion, performances, and public work. The setting does not represent a specific cultural tradition.

## Two shows and four opportunities

A and B each have one personal show. The stage is unbuilt, neither show performed, and the wrap-up incomplete. Play two rounds, A then B taking one action each, for four total. Everything is public, with no payment or randomness.

Either may build the stage once. Once built, each may perform their own show once, never the other's. After both shows, either may complete the shared wrap-up once. Each may also promote their own show once for 1 promotion marker, which changes no stage, performance, or wrap-up state. Waiting is allowed.

Each option takes one action; building and performing cannot be combined. Reselect unmet prerequisites or repeated completed actions without spending an opportunity. After action four, both shows performed and the wrap-up complete mean shared success; otherwise failure. Promotion is recorded separately and cannot replace missing public tasks. There is no individual ranking.

## Make the show visible first

A promotes, then B builds. In round two A performs A's show and B performs B's. Both shows finish with 1 promotion marker, but no wrap-up: **failure**.

This does not establish that promotion has no value. Within this four-action window, it occupies an opportunity required by another task. The current goal does not let apparent visibility substitute for completing the shared work.

## Put public work into the complete sequence

Reset. A builds, B performs; in round two A performs, then B handles the wrap-up.

| Action / player | Promotion first | Plan public work |
|---|---|---|
| 1 / A | Promote | Build |
| 2 / B | Build | Perform B |
| 3 / A | Perform A | Perform A |
| 4 / B | Perform B | Wrap up |
| Final | Two shows, 1 promotion, no wrap-up | Two shows, 0 promotion, wrap-up done |

The second **succeeds**. Each still acts twice. A builds and B wraps up: the supporting work has been distributed rather than erased.

## See the output and the actions supporting it

The organizers bring personal shows but also perform public work under a shared time limit. Performance count is not the sole completion condition; supporting actions must actually occur.

If promotion should affect attendance, specify how and whether that changes preparation or wrap-up needs. A dedicated backstage role would require a new allocation of opportunities and authority. Shared victory here does not establish that real organizers would consider the workload fair.

## Check and model boundary

Change only the action limit to five, giving A the extra action. Can the first sequence recover?

**Yes.** Both shows are finished after action four, so A can wrap up on five and succeed in the variant. More time also gives A another task to perform. It is not a solution without another changed consequence.

Promotion markers are not measured audiences, and building and wrapping up are not real event instructions. The model puts visibility, opportunity, and public labor into one record.

# Fantasy: why can't you deliver the token after teleporting to the tower?

Fantasy can permit actions impossible in reality. Designers still need to specify what an ability changes, what it costs, and which restrictions remain. This original delivery episode does not require a warrior: its magic creates a relationship between a shortcut and a later expense.

## One courier and four actions

The bidirectional route is Camp 0—Path 1—Tower 2. Start at 0 carrying a token and 2 mana. A closed ward at the tower prevents delivery, not arrival. Everything is public, with no enemies, dice, or other players.

Take at most four actions. Each chooses one option: walk one adjacent connection without spending mana; teleport from any location to 2 for 2 mana; open the ward while at 2 for 1 mana; deliver the token at 2 if the ward is open, without spending mana; or wait. Each option costs one action.

Mana never replenishes. The token always travels with the courier, including during teleportation. There is no dropping, trading, or additional ability. Open the ward only once; it remains open. Reselect an unavailable or unaffordable action without spending it. Mana cannot go negative.

Delivery wins immediately. Otherwise fail after action four. There are no arrival points, leftover-mana points, or extra resolution steps.

## Arrive quickly without the opening cost

Teleport on action one, spending both mana. The token arrives too, but the ward remains closed. You can neither pay to open it nor deliver through it. Arrival does not automatically remove the ward.

Wait on actions two, three, and four: failure. Replacing waits with walking back toward camp would not help, because no action restores mana. This sequence establishes that **quick arrival does not satisfy every delivery condition**.

## Walk and retain the mana

Reset. Walk from 0 to 1 on action one, then to 2 on action two. Pay 1 mana to open the ward on action three, and deliver on action four. Success, with 1 mana left.

| Sequence | Arrival | Mana on arrival | Opening and delivery | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Teleport | Action 1 | 0 | Cannot pay to open | Failure |
| Walk | Action 2 | 2 | Open on 3; deliver on 4 | Success |

Arriving one action later preserves the resource needed afterward. Magic is not a button that must be stronger because of the setting. Under this goal and cost, teleportation is not a successful shortcut.

## Make the world's abilities shareable

The player is a courier carrying a token. Actions include ordinary travel, magical travel, and opening a ward. Two uses compete for mana. Teleportation changes location without changing the ward; opening changes permission to deliver without completing delivery.

To make teleportation an attractive viable option, change its cost, mana sources, or the task. Specify the change in advance. Inventing immunity to magic only after arrival would not give readers the same basis for comparison as a visible restriction.

## Simplifications and the next observation

There is no combat, character progression, role-play dialogue, or free-form adjudication. Two mana support this local decision; not every fantasy game needs such a system. The rules describe no other magic, and a name alone does not grant further powers.

Ask a reader what teleportation carried and what it left unchanged. If they assume it also opens the ward, examine the ability wording and ward marker before adding more world history.

## Check: teleportation costs only 1 mana

Change only that cost from 2 to 1. Starting mana remains 2; retain every other rule.

**Delivery can now succeed on action three.** Teleport on one, leaving 1 mana; open on two, leaving 0; deliver on three. This saves one walking action, not the opening or delivery actions. It is an explicit variant, not an ability already present in the base rules.

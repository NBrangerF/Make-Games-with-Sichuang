# Restoring an occupied building: both rooms repaired, but usable throughout?

A building that remains in use has more to its restoration than final completion markers. Which spaces stay available during work can become a condition players must maintain. This develops the existing restoration setting through two rooms and two work schedules.

## Two rooms and four work actions

One coordinator manages rooms A and B, each connected directly to the entrance hall. Both start open and unrepaired. Take four actions. Start work on an open, unrepaired room to close it immediately; finish a room under construction to reopen it and mark it repaired; or wait.

Each uses one action, with no material cost, random outcome, or additional stage. A repaired room cannot be started again, an unstarted room cannot be finished, and one action cannot repair both. Reselect an invalid action without spending it.

**After every action**, record whether at least one room is open to meet that moment's usage requirement. If neither is open, record an unmet requirement but continue through action four. Success requires both rooms repaired and all four usage checks passed. Otherwise fail. There is no rule exchanging downtime for extra points.

All states are public. The player chooses how to arrange two starts and two finishes, but “under construction” never counts as open.

## Start both first: a good ending with an interruption

Start A, start B, finish A, finish B. The open-room counts are **1, 0, 1, 2**. Both rooms are repaired at the end, but none was usable after action two. Overall failure.

No surprise event punishes the work. Continued use was a declared goal, and an interruption remains in the record rather than being canceled by two rooms reopening later.

## Finish one before starting the next

Reset, then start A, finish A, start B, finish B.

| Action | Work | Open rooms | Usage check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Start A | 1 | Pass |
| 2 | Finish A | 2 | Pass |
| 3 | Start B | 1 | Pass |
| 4 | Finish B | 2 | Pass |

The counts are **1, 2, 1, 2**. Both repairs and every check succeed. No extra work opportunities or altered targets were needed; closures were staggered.

## How do users' needs continually affect construction?

The player schedules work while the rules examine availability after each step. This places a usage requirement inside the consequences rather than merely saying that people still occupy the building. Unlike final adjacency scoring in the city-building article, this retains a record of the whole process.

The coordinator represents one explicit requirement; residents have no negotiation actions themselves. A design about relocation, room changes, or residents changing arrangements would need corresponding rights and costs, rather than treating residents only as random obstacles.

## Check and model boundary

Both rooms are under construction. A will finish on the next action. Can the current usage check count it as open?

**No.** A reopens only after its finishing action resolves. Future completion does not replace its current state. This is a separate question from whether both rooms can eventually be repaired.

The two-room layout and one-step starts and finishes deliberately omit actual building safety, duration, materials, and restoration techniques. The model distinguishes final achievements from conditions maintained along the way; it does not evaluate real construction plans.

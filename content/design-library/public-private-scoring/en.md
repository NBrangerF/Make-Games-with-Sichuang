# Public and personal scoring: who knows the goal, and who can score it?

“This is my goal” describes scoring eligibility, not necessarily secrecy. “This is a public goal” does not tell us whether its reward is available once or to everyone. This overview separates goal ownership, information visibility, eligibility, and scoring time, then combines them in one local phase.

## Write four separate answers

In VIC-06, Geoffrey Engelstein and Isaac Shalev distinguish personal from public end-game goals, and goals that multiple players can score from competitive awards. VIC-05 discusses whether victory-point totals themselves are public. Hiding points already earned and hiding a condition for future points are different information arrangements.

Rules need to answer: who owns the goal, who can read its condition now, whether every qualifying player scores, and when and how often it is checked. “Private” can mean either personal eligibility or knowledge restricted to one player. Below, “personal goal” and “hidden goal” keep those meanings separate.

## Two players, four rounds, four goals

This is an original panel-making phase. Lin and Qiao each start with 4 effort tokens and no panels, calibration markers, or points. Shared supply contains 8 panels and 4 calibration markers. There are four rounds, with Lin acting before Qiao each round; each player takes four actions. All materials in play, actions, and awarded points are public.

Each action requires paying 1 effort into that player's spent area, then choosing: build, taking 1 panel from supply; calibrate, placing 1 marker on one of their own uncalibrated panels; or wait, gaining nothing. A player with no panel cannot calibrate, and a panel cannot be calibrated twice. Costs and supplies must be available. There is no trading, removal, borrowing, changing goals, or taking additional actions.

| Goal | Who can read it | Who scores, and when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First to two panels | Both players from the start | The first player to reach 2 panels automatically gains 3 points at the end of that action; awarded once in the phase |
| Calibration display | Both players from the start | At phase end, each player with at least 1 calibrated panel gains 2 points |
| Lin's production goal | Face up in front of Lin; both can read it | At phase end, only Lin gains 4 points for having at least 3 panels |
| Qiao's personal goal | Qiao reads it first; revealed at the end | Only Qiao can earn its 5 points, under the condition below |

Qiao draws one of two shuffled goal cards: one requires at least 2 calibrated panels, the other at least 4 panels. Both possible conditions are public, but only Qiao reads the drawn card before the first action. The other card remains face down and unexamined. Until the reveal, players may not show, disclose, or ask about the private card. In both worked routes, Qiao's actual card is fixed as “at least two calibrated panels.”

Check the first-to-two goal after each action's effect, without an additional effort cost. The specified turn order prevents simultaneous claims. If nobody qualifies, no award is made. Its 3 points are permanent; another player later having more panels cannot take them away. Check all other goals once after all eight actions: reveal Qiao's card, then count 1 base point per panel, plus the first-to-two award and achieved goals. Awards add together and consume no panels or markers. Failed goals score 0 with no penalty. Final ties remain ties.

## Route one: Qiao completes the calibration goal

Lin's four actions are build, calibrate, build, build. Qiao's are build, calibrate, build, calibrate. After round one, each has one panel. Each calibrates that panel in round two. In round three, Lin builds a second panel first and immediately receives 3 points. Qiao then also has two panels, but the first-to-two award has already been claimed.

In round four, Lin builds a third panel and Qiao calibrates the second. Lin ends with 3 panels, 1 calibrated; Qiao has 2 panels, both calibrated. Each player's 4 effort tokens have been paid. Supply retains 3 panels and 1 calibration marker.

At the end, Lin receives 3 base points, 3 first-to-two points, 2 public calibration points, and 4 personal production points, totaling 12. Qiao receives 2 base points, 0 first-to-two points, 2 public calibration points, and 5 personal-goal points, totaling 9. Each receives the full 2-point public calibration award; they do not divide one shared reward.

## Route two: Qiao builds every time

Reset the initial state, goal card, and turn order. Lin repeats the same four actions, while Qiao builds on all four turns. In round two, Lin calibrates while still having one panel. Qiao then completes a second panel and claims the 3 points first. When Lin reaches two panels in round three, the award is not given again.

Lin again ends with 3 panels, 1 calibrated; Qiao has 4 panels, none calibrated. Both players have spent all their effort. Supply retains 1 panel and 3 calibration markers. Despite having four panels, Qiao's actual card requires two calibrated panels. Qiao cannot score using the card that was not drawn.

| Route and player | Panel base points | First-to-two | Public calibration | Own goal | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Calibration route: Lin | 3 | 3 | 2 | 4 | 12 |
| Calibration route: Qiao | 2 | 0 | 2 | 5 | 9 |
| All-build route: Lin | 3 | 0 | 2 | 4 | 9 |
| All-build route: Qiao | 4 | 3 | 0 | 0 | 7 |

Qiao's change transfers the first-to-two award and gives up both calibration rewards. This compares specified actions; it does not establish Qiao's optimal choice. Lin's behavior, goals, and resources were held fixed.

## Change only when Qiao's goal becomes public

Now move the reveal of Qiao's drawn card from phase end to before the first action. Keep its owner, condition, 5-point award, end-of-phase check, and all actions unchanged. Replaying the two routes still produces 12/9 and 9/7. Seeing Qiao's card does not make Lin eligible to collect its 5 points.

The difference is information. Under the original rules, consider the instant after the last action but before revealing the card. In route one, Lin sees that Qiao has two calibrated panels and can establish 4 points from panels and the public goal. The personal bonus could be 0 or 5, leaving possible totals of 4 or 9. In route two, the possible totals are 7 or 12. Lin can guess which card Qiao holds, but the rules do not require Qiao to act toward a personal goal. Repeated building alone cannot rule out the calibration card.

With the earlier reveal, Lin knows from the start that the actual card is the calibration goal. In those same final states, Qiao's totals are known to be 9 and 7 respectively. Changed information need not change the score of a fixed action replay. Whether people change their choices because of it requires separate observation.

## Check: does seeing a goal make you eligible?

1. **Qiao's personal card is face up, and Lin also has two calibrated panels. Can Lin claim its 5 points?** No. Only visibility changed; eligibility was not extended to everyone.
2. **What if both players build, calibrate, wait, then wait?** Each has one panel, calibrated, and scores 1 + 2 = 3. Neither personal goal is achieved, the first-to-two award is unclaimed, and the final result is a 3/3 tie. All eight effort tokens are still spent.
3. **With four panels, is Qiao certain to gain the personal 5 points before the reveal?** No. There are two candidate cards. The actual calibration card scores 0 in that state. Qiao finishes with 7 in this example; 12 is the other outcome not yet ruled out before the reveal.

# Read rules independently: what is missing from “unload books”?

A designer saying “obviously, one at a time” can unblock a session without proving that the written rules are complete. Independent reading should identify the missing decision and preserve it in the materials.

## Start with an explicit reference version

This is this site's original *Book Cart* v1.0, separate from the main course's river-crossing example. A and B share one cart, with A acting first and turns alternating. The cart starts empty at Depot 0. The two-way route is Depot 0—Door 1—Reading Corner 2. Two books start at the depot; the cart holds at most two. All information is public.

There are at most six actions in total. Each turn chooses one legal action: load one book at the depot; move to an adjacent location; unload one book at the reading corner; or wait. Each uses one action. Movement does not automatically load or unload. Books can only be loaded at the depot and unloaded at the corner; delivered books cannot be collected again. An unavailable action must be reselected without spending an action. Discussion is allowed; the active player decides.

After each action, both players immediately win if both books have been unloaded at the corner. Otherwise, both lose after action six. There are no individual points, random events, or extra actions.

[Download the English print-and-play: rules, board, and complete variant comparison](/print-and-play/book-cart/en.pdf). The worked example below is complete without printing.

The complete reference says “unload one book at the reading corner.” Below, we deliberately remove the quantity to create a draft fragment for examination. The printable file does not use this incomplete wording.

## Two reasonable interpretations of the same draft

Place the cart at the reading corner with two books aboard and four actions already used. The draft says only, “At the reading corner, you may unload books. Choose one action each turn.” It does not specify the number unloaded per action.

| Meaning supplied by the reader | After action five | Completion |
|---|---|---|
| Unload one book | One delivered, one still aboard | Unload again on action six |
| Unload everything | Both delivered, cart empty | Immediate win on action five |

Neither reader needs to make an arithmetic error to reach a different ending. The missing quantity causes the divergence, rather than failure to follow a shared complete rule. Record the disputed wording and the position that exposed it.

## What do an oral answer and a written revision each preserve?

First process: the designer says “one book at a time,” and the reader unloads across two actions. The explanation lets this session proceed. A later reader receiving only the old draft may still not know.

Second process: revise the sentence to “With the cart at the reading corner and at least one book aboard, spend one action to unload exactly one book. Any remaining book needs another action.” Recreate the two-book position after action four and let a new reader judge using only the revised materials. The expected answer is completion on action six; actual observation is still needed before reporting that a reader understood.

Also retain the ending order: check shared success after each action; only if unsuccessful after action six do players lose. Otherwise, finishing the delivery on the last action can introduce another conflict between success and expiry.

## Preserve a usable independent-reading record

Record the material version, state before the question, interpretation chosen, any oral help, and the exact revised sentence. Help can be provided when needed; mark that point as dependent on an oral explanation. A test does not require leaving someone stuck indefinitely.

Before checking again, establish which version the reader has. Someone who already knows the answer may expose obvious contradictions, but their success does not establish that unfamiliar readers can understand independently. The distinction concerns the test's purpose, not grading the reader.

## Check: would testing with one book expose the gap?

With the cart at the corner carrying only one book, both interpretations unload it in one action. **That position cannot distinguish “one” from “all.”** Choose a state where competing interpretations produce different results.

Another question: under the revised rules, the second book is unloaded on action six. Win or lose? **Win.** Check completed delivery first, then apply the deadline only if it is incomplete.

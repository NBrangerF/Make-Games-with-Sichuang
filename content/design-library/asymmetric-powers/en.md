# Asymmetric powers: with equal resources, which action rules differ?

Two players with 2 energy each need not be able to accomplish the same things. One might save a movement action; another might waive a delivery fee. An asymmetric power needs to specify who may change a shared rule, how, and under what conditions, beyond giving characters different names.

## Separate powers, resources, information, and roles

In ACT-12, “Variable Player Powers,” Geoffrey Engelstein and Isaac Shalev discuss exclusive actions and changes to ordinary actions or resolution procedures. This entry focuses on that ability-level scope.

Placing one additional energy at setup is an initial-resource difference. Letting only one player see an order is an information difference. Different roles can also change objectives or narrative identity. These arrangements can coexist with powers, but should be specified separately. If only the names differ while available actions and costs are identical, there is no power difference of the kind examined here.

## Two couriers on one route

This is an original local phase for two players. There are three locations in a line: warehouse 0—near station 1—far station 2. Lin and Qiao both start at 0 with 1 parcel, 2 energy, and 0 points each. The near station has one public 3-point contract, and the far station one public 6-point contract. Each can be completed only once. The energy bank and delivered-parcel area begin empty.

There are three rounds. Lin takes one action before Qiao in each round. Ordinary actions are: move to an adjacent location for 1 energy; deliver at the near or far station with an uncompleted contract by paying 1 energy and surrendering 1 personal parcel, gaining the contract's points; or wait at no cost and with no effect. Pay first, place the parcel in the delivered area, and move the contract in front of its completer. Delivery is illegal without the fee, parcel, or available contract.

Players may share a location. There is no trading, borrowing energy, or obtaining another parcel. Moving and delivering are separate actions: arriving at or passing a station does not complete a contract. All spent energy enters the bank and is not replenished into players' supplies. Finish after both actions in round three, even if contracts were completed earlier; this neither adds nor cancels slots. The highest contract-point total wins, with ties shared. Remaining energy, parcels, and position score nothing.

Both powers are public from the start and remain with their owners. Lin's “Long Stride” can be used once per phase to move two consecutive edges along the line in one movement action, without reversing direction, still paying 1 energy. Moving the full two edges uses the power: pay and mark it used before moving. Qiao's “Delivery Fee Waiver” changes the energy cost of Qiao's delivery from 1 to 0. It still requires an action, a parcel, and an uncompleted contract. Movement costs remain unchanged.

Lin can therefore stride from 0 to 2, but cannot do so indefinitely. Qiao can deliver with no energy, but not with no parcel. Neither can use the other's power.

## Route one: Lin goes far, Qiao goes near

In round one, Lin strides to 2, paying 1 energy and retaining 1. Qiao moves normally to 1, also paying 1 and retaining 1. Passing the near station did not give Lin its contract; both remain available.

In round two, Lin pays the last 1 energy and hands over the parcel to complete the far contract for 6 points. Qiao uses the waiver to deliver the parcel at no energy cost, completing the near contract for 3 points. Both wait in round three.

Lin finishes at 2 and Qiao at 1, with energy 0/1 and both parcels delivered. The bank holds 3 energy and the delivered area 2 parcels; the contracts belong to Lin and Qiao respectively. Lin wins 6 to 3. The stride covered the far route in one action; the waiver left Qiao one energy, but remaining energy does not score in this phase.

## Route two: Lin completes the near contract first

Return to the same initial state. In round one, both move normally to 1, paying 1 energy each and retaining 1. They may share the near station. Lin has not used the stride.

In round two, Lin pays the last 1 energy and delivers the parcel for the near contract's 3 points. Qiao cannot complete that same contract again, so spends the last 1 energy to move normally to 2. Lin waits in round three. Despite having 0 energy, Qiao can use the waiver to deliver the parcel for the far contract's 6 points.

Lin finishes at 1 and Qiao at 2. Both have 0 energy and no parcel; the bank holds 4 energy and the delivered area 2 parcels. Qiao wins 6 to 3. Lin's unused stride grants no automatic bonus. The two routes show conditions in which each power matters; they do not establish overall balance between the characters.

## Raise only the price of Lin's stride

Now change only the stride's energy cost from 1 to 2. Ordinary movement and Qiao's power remain unchanged. Keep route one's plan: Lin strides to the far station, delivers in round two if legal and otherwise waits, then waits in round three. Qiao still moves to the near station, delivers, and waits. The “wait if delivery is unaffordable” choice is the same in both versions.

In round one, Lin can pay 2 energy to stride, but arrives with 0. In round two, Lin lacks the 1 energy required for delivery and chooses to wait, retaining the parcel and gaining no points. Qiao still waives the fee and completes the near contract for 3 points. Both wait in the final round.

| Complete route | Energy remaining: Lin / Qiao | Parcels remaining | Contract points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lin far, Qiao near | 0 / 1 | 0 / 0 | 6 / 3 |
| Lin near, Qiao far | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 | 3 / 6 |
| Stride costs 2, first plan retained | 0 / 1 | 1 / 0 | 0 / 3 |

In the changed-price version, the bank still holds 3 energy, but the delivered area contains only Qiao's 1 parcel. Lin retains 1 parcel, and the far contract remains uncompleted. Positions match route one, but the result differs: reaching a location and being able to pay for the next action are separate conditions.

This comparison identifies the stride fee's consequences only under these budgets, action counts, and contracts. Assessing balance would require changing initiative, distances, rewards, resources, and opposing choices, alongside observing actual play. One win or an exchange of scores is not proof.

## Check: what exactly does the power waive?

1. **Qiao is at the far station with 0 energy and 1 parcel, and its contract remains available. Is delivery legal?** Yes. The energy fee is waived. The parcel, action, and available-contract requirements remain. Qiao does not gain 1 energy as a refund.
2. **Lin has just taken the near contract. Can Qiao, at the same location, deliver for another 3 points?** No. There was one contract and it is completed. Sharing a location does not create another contract.
3. **After using the stride, Lin still has 1 energy. Can Lin stride back from 2 to 0?** No. Affording the fee does not restore a use. Lin may still choose a legal ordinary move.
4. **Who wins if both wait in all three rounds?** Both score 0 and tie. Each retains 2 energy and 1 parcel, and both public contracts remain uncompleted.

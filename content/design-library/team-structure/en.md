# Team structure: winning together, deciding separately

A team structure first answers who receives victory: a group of players competes against other groups as a team. “We win together” still leaves open who chooses actions, who spends resources, and who knows a partner’s choice. Those rules determine whether cooperation involves discussion, divided responsibilities, or coordination with limited clues.

STR-03 in the second edition of *Building Blocks of Tabletop Game Design* treats competition between teams as a game structure and discusses team formation, communication, and differentiated roles. Sharing resources is one possible arrangement. The one-round transport game below is an original teaching example that separates these questions. It does not reproduce a commercial game or a complete play history.

## Complete rules for one delivery round

Four players form two public, fixed teams. Red has Lin as navigator and Qiao as loader; Blue has Han as navigator and Yue as loader. Each navigator holds two route cards, North and South. Each loader holds three choice cards, North Kit, South Kit, and No Load, plus 2 energy tokens. There are 10 choice cards and 4 energy tokens in total. The bank starts empty. There are no other resources or random events.

Everyone knows the rules, starting resources, and teams. After setup, players may not talk, signal, or show cards. Each player chooses only their own card, cannot make a partner’s decision, and cannot transfer or borrow energy. Resolve the following sequence.

1. Both navigators secretly select and simultaneously lock one route card. Only its owner knows the choice, which can no longer change.
2. Both loaders secretly select and lock one card. North Kit costs 2 energy, South Kit costs 1, and No Load costs 0. A loader must be able to pay the full cost from their own energy. Payment waits until the reveal so its amount does not disclose the choice. No other actions or resource changes occur in this interval.
3. All four players reveal together. Loaders immediately pay their full costs to the bank, including for a mismatched kit. There are no refunds.
4. A team qualifies for a route’s reward only if its route and kit both name North or both name South. No Load never qualifies. North awards 8 team points and South awards 6. One qualifying team takes the whole reward; two qualifying teams split that route’s reward equally; a route with no qualifying team awards nothing.
5. Each team adds 1 team point per energy token its loader has left. Both members of the higher-scoring team win together. Equal totals mean a draw for both teams and all four players. The game ends immediately, with no further actions, individual ranking, or additional rewards.

An unqualified team does not share or reduce another team’s reward. Energy moves only from loaders to the bank, conserving all 4 tokens. Points are generated during scoring; they are not payment currency.

## Two complete outcomes: who bears a mismatch?

Both sequences below are legal. Keep Lin on North, Han on South, and Yue on South Kit; change only Qiao’s actual choice. Under the base rules Qiao cannot see Lin’s card, so the first sequence does not mean Qiao already knew the correct answer.

| Sequence | Red’s settlement | Blue’s settlement | Final result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Successful coordination: Qiao picks North Kit | North matches North Kit; pay 2, receive all 8 North points, retain 0 energy: total 8 | South matches South Kit; pay 1, receive all 6 South points, retain 1 energy: total 7 | Both Red players win; bank 3, Qiao 0, Yue 1 |
| Mismatch: Qiao picks South Kit | North does not match South Kit; pay 1, receive no route reward, retain 1 energy: total 1 | South matches South Kit; pay 1. Red is unqualified, so Blue still receives all 6 points and retains 1 energy: total 7 | Both Blue players win; bank 2, Qiao 1, Yue 1 |

Buying South Kit does not automatically reroute Red to South. Authority to lock a route and authority to choose a kit are separate. Saving 1 energy does not replace the lost 8-point route reward: this particular choice lowers Red’s total from 8 to 1.

The loss appears in the team score, and Lin and Qiao share it. The rules do not assign a separate individual defeat according to who made the mistake. Nor does Yue win more than Han because Yue retained energy. Team membership determines who shares the result; it does not automatically merge everyone’s action permissions.

## Change one rule: show a partner the route earlier

In the variant, after both navigators lock their routes but before loaders choose, each navigator shows the locked route only to their own loader. Opponents still cannot see it, and discussion remains forbidden. Routes cannot change. Loaders still make their own choices and pay their own costs. Rewards and victory conditions remain identical.

To compare the information itself, give both loaders the same fixed decision policy: “If my team’s route is visible to me, choose its matching kit; otherwise choose South Kit.” Again, Lin locks North and Han locks South. In the base version both loaders choose South Kit, producing Red 1 and Blue 7. In the variant Qiao sees North and chooses North Kit, while Yue still chooses South Kit, producing Red 8 and Blue 7. Total energy payments are 2 and 3 respectively; there is no free compensation.

Earlier disclosure lets this policy coordinate successfully, but does not give Lin control of Qiao’s action. Qiao may still legally choose a mismatched kit or No Load. Both teams might also match North with North Kit and receive 4 points each. Seeing a partner’s route reveals nothing about the opposing route and does not guarantee an exclusive reward. Permission to see the route only after everyone reveals comes too late to change a choice.

## Uses and boundaries

Team structures are useful when players should hold different responsibilities while sharing the outcome of a competition. Check separately how teams form, when communication is allowed, who makes each final decision, whether resources can move, and whether rewards belong to individuals or teams. Our inference from the example is that these permissions help identify whether a mismatch comes from missing information, conflicting decisions, or resource arrangements. Simply asking players to cooperate cannot distinguish those causes.

Teams need not be equal in size or fixed throughout play. Public pairs are only one arrangement. When the whole table competes against a system, the result covers every player; the cooperative-structure entry develops that case. Hidden information concerns who knows what and can occur within or between teams. A traitor structure also involves conflicting faction objectives: temporarily hiding a route from a teammate does not establish betrayal. Continue through the related entries below.

Limiting communication may give each player more judgment to exercise, or make mismatches feel arbitrary. Discussion may help coordination, or allow an experienced player to dominate recommendations. These are experiences to observe; a team label alone guarantees neither participation nor fairness.

## Self-checks and answers

- **What if both teams choose North and North Kit?** Each pays 2 and receives half of North’s reward: 4 points, with no energy left. The bank holds 4 and the teams draw. Revealing first does not grant priority over the full 8-point reward.
- **What if both choose South and South Kit?** Each pays 1, receives 3 route points, and adds 1 for retained energy: another 4–4 draw. The bank holds 2 and each loader retains 1.
- **Both navigators choose North, Qiao chooses No Load, and Yue chooses North Kit. Must they split North’s reward?** No. Red is unqualified and scores 2 from retained energy. Blue receives all 8 points and retains 0 energy. Blue wins; the bank holds 2.
- **Suppose Qiao has only 1 energy, the other token is already in the bank, and Yue still has 2. Can Lin choose to buy North Kit for Qiao?** No. Qiao must personally hold 2 energy to choose North Kit and must make that choice. Seeing the route, sharing a team, or having enough energy across the table creates no borrowing or payment permission.

# Negotiation and commitments: what does a three-party bond actually secure?

“I will support you if you pay” joins actions at different times. Money can move now while support remains a later choice. Recording a promise, posting a bond, and forbidding a change of action modify different rules. This site organizes those questions as the design pattern “negotiation and commitments,” not as a synonym for one BGG mechanism.

## One three-party proposal, five explicit commitments

This is an original, complete local sequence, not a complete match history. A, B, and C each have 4 coins and 0 points. The bank has 18, making 30 coins in total. Resources and rewards are public. There are no hands, random events, or hidden objectives. Compare individual results without extra ranking rewards; equal final totals are ties.

A, then B, then C may each speak once for at most 30 seconds, suggesting fees for A and B to pay C. A may then write one final proposal or make none. It must specify separate whole-number fees of 0 to 2 coins from A and B, plus three later promises: A will build, B will build, and C will support. All three simultaneously accept or refuse. Only unanimous acceptance forms an agreement; there is no further bargaining. Other assets, side agreements, and future rounds cannot be included.

Before implementing an accepted proposal, check every immediate payment: A and B can afford their respective fees, and C can post 2 coins from holdings before receiving those fees. Only if every check passes are the bond locked in a separate area and the fees transferred as one transaction. Otherwise there is no agreement and no money moves. A's and B's fees go immediately to C and are not automatically refunded for a later breach. Refusal or no proposal means no fees or bond, but play still proceeds to the action stage.

Each player secretly selects one action; all reveal and execute together. A and B may each build, paying 2 coins to the bank, or take a short job, freely receiving 2 coins from the bank. C may support for free and earn 3 points, or do private work for free and earn 6. Building awards 7 points to each builder only if both A and B build and C supports. Otherwise any player who chose to build earns just 1 point and still pays the cost. Players must be able to afford their chosen action. A promise does not remove short jobs or private work from the legal choices. Everyone has exactly this one action.

Resolve the bond last. With an agreement and actual support from C, return the whole bond to C. If C does private work, divide it equally between A and B. It secures **only C's selection of support**, not A's or B's building choices or successful construction. A or B taking a short job incurs no additional fine. Without an agreement, there is no bond to resolve. End immediately afterward. Add remaining coins to points, empty the bond area, and make no later claims or reputation awards.

## The same agreement, followed through or breached

Fix unanimous acceptance of a proposal charging A 2 and B 2. C posts 2, then receives 4, holding 6 coins. A and B each retain 2. The bank still has 18, and the bond area holds 2.

Route one: A and B build, and C supports. A and B each spend their remaining 2 and earn 7 points. C earns 3 and receives the bond back, holding 8 coins for a final 11. Results are 7 / 7 / 11. The bank holds 22 and the players 8, preserving 30.

Route two: A and B still build, but C does private work. Each builder still spends 2 but earns only 1 point. The two bond coins compensate them by one coin each, giving each a final 2. C retains 6 coins and earns 6 points, finishing at 12. Results are 2 / 2 / 12. The bank still has 22; individual coins are 1 / 1 / 6.

| Same accepted proposal, 2-coin bond | A's result | B's result | C's result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Build / build / support | 7 | 7 | 11 |
| Build / build / private work | 2 | 2 | 12 |

C forfeits the bond but gains 1 final point given the other two actions. The forfeiture compensates A and B without covering their individual falls from 7 to 2. It does not make support inevitable.

## Change only the bond, from 2 to 4

Reset the start, retaining the same fees and acceptance. Change only the required bond to 4 coins. C has 4 and can lock them before receiving the fees, then holds 4 after payment. Actions, rewards, returns, and equal division remain unchanged.

If everyone follows through, C receives the 4 back and results remain 7 / 7 / 11. If C does private work, A and B receive 2 each: each earns 1 point plus 2 coins, while C earns 6 points plus 4 coins. Results are 3 / 3 / 10. The bank still holds 22; individual coins of 2 / 2 / 4 preserve the total of 30.

Considering only C's final total and fixing A and B as builders, the larger bond makes support at 11 better than private work at 10. Private work remains legal. Players may consider relationships absent from these rules or decline to maximize points. A numerical incentive is not a performance guarantee. Forcing support through the rules would require another change to legal actions or automatic execution.

## Scope: separate what is promised from what is secured

The proposal negotiates two immediate payments alongside three future actions, but only one action by C carries a bond. Specify amounts, execution times, records, breach conditions, and recipients of forfeited security to establish what agreement means. Bond coins are temporarily unavailable to spend. Fees have already changed hands. They are not one freely refundable deposit.

This example deliberately contains a single action stage, so it cannot measure reputation in repeated cooperation. Actual players may revise trust based on past conduct, and a game could separately provide public performance records. This example supplies neither such observations nor rewards. The book's discussion of non-binding promises engaging feelings about honesty and loyalty does not establish that every player responds alike.

Negotiation can help coordinate several players' actions. It is adjacent to immediate trading, but action promises need their own enforcement scope. Discussion windows and bond markers also impose memory and handling costs; we have measured neither participant time nor experience here.

## Checks: four boundaries before approving a proposal

1. **With no agreement, what happens after short job / short job / private work?** A and B each have 6 coins for 6 points. C has 4 coins plus 6 points for 10. The bank has 14. Compared with this route, full performance of the two-coin-fee proposal produces 7 / 7 / 11, a gain of 1 each. That does not prove acceptance or performance.
2. **With a 4-coin bond, what if A takes a short job, B builds, and C supports?** A has 2 after paying the fee, receives 2, and finishes at 4. B's failed build earns 1 with no coins remaining. C supported and receives the whole bond back, still finishing at 11. Results are 4 / 1 / 11. A's breach cannot forfeit C's bond.
3. **Can a 6-coin bond be posted after C receives the fees?** Not under these rules. C has only 4 before receiving payment. The check fails, so nobody pays fees or moves a bond. Allowing receipts to fund security would require a separate change to payment timing.
4. **If C does private work, can A and B recover their fees and split the bond too?** No. Immediate fees are not refunded. Only the bond is divided as specified. Returning the fees too would add a refund and alter the results above.

## Sources and classification scope

Geoffrey Engelstein and Isaac Shalev, [*Building Blocks of Tabletop Game Design*, second edition](https://doi.org/10.1201/9781003179184), ECO-18 “Negotiation,” printed pages 348–349, distinguishes agreements about actions from resource trading and discusses binding scope, discussion stages, and non-binding promises. ECO-02 “Trading,” printed pages 310–311, discusses eligible assets, trading windows, and enforcement problems with future promises. The three-party proposal, bond system, and numerical comparisons are original analysis, not translated book rules.

[BGG: Negotiation](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgamemechanic/2915/negotiation) is used only as a verified related classification identity. Specific promises still depend on the rules for execution. This classification is not equivalent to this site's pattern covering negotiation, records, security, and the scope of performance obligations.

# Area control: who owns a place after the piece leaves?

Where a piece stands, who controls that place, and what control provides are three separate rule questions. They may coincide, but need not. Calling an action “occupation” does not determine what happens after the piece leaves.

## Separate the forms of control

This is an overview. Some rules assign an area to at most one player. Others allow several players to coexist and compare their influence. Other rules let a piece affect nearby spaces. A map and military-looking pieces do not establish one shared rule system.

Our example compares **retaining an ownership marker** with **requiring a piece to remain**. It contains no combat and does not use area-majority scoring. The area-majority entry provides a separate example of that relationship.

## Cove, Square, and Hill

This original two-player local phase has public information. Three areas form a line: **Cove—Square—Hill**. Movement is only between adjacent areas.

A starts with one person piece at Cove; B has one at Hill. Square is empty. Cove has A's ownership marker, Hill has B's, and Square has none. Both players have 0 points. At phase end, Cove, Square, and Hill award their controllers 2, 1, and 3 points respectively.

A takes one action, then B takes one. Each action is either a free move into an adjacent area without the other player's person, or a wait. No skipping areas, swapping people, or combat is allowed. Entering an area immediately changes its ownership marker to yours. **The area you leave retains its marker.** People do not automatically return after scoring.

After B's action, score each area once according to its ownership marker, then end. An unmarked area scores nothing. We compare points for this phase without assuming a larger game's victory condition.

## Two legal sequences

| Sequence | Final people | Markers: Cove / Square / Hill | Final points: A / B |
|---|---|---|---|
| A moves to Square; B waits | A at Square, B at Hill | A / A / B | 3 / 3 |
| A waits; B moves to Square | A at Cove, B at Square | A / B / B | 2 / 4 |

In the first sequence, A retains Cove's 2 points after leaving and gains Square's 1. B cannot enter Square while A's person occupies it, so waits. A controls two places without standing in both.

In the second, B retains Hill's 3 points and adds Square's 1. These are specified legal sequences. They do not assume B would choose the same action under every scoring rule.

## Change only control after departure

Reset the initial position. Change one rule: **your person must remain to maintain control; remove an area's ownership marker when its person leaves**. Movement permissions, turn order, area values, and scoring time stay unchanged.

Repeat the first sequence. A leaves Cove for Square, removing Cove's marker. B waits. A receives only Square's 1 point; B receives Hill's 3: a result of 1 / 3. The original 3 / 3 becomes 1 / 3 because Cove's 2 points are no longer retained.

Repeat the second sequence. A waits and B moves to Square. Hill becomes uncontrolled, producing 2 / 1. B could instead remain at Hill: both waiting gives 2 / 3. If B seeks only their own phase points, staying at Hill beats advancing to Square. Do not insist that B will advance merely to preserve a demonstration sequence.

## Connect control to a specific effect

Our example connects control to final scoring and uses physical occupation to block entry. These are different relationships. In the original version, empty Cove remains A-controlled, but the person, not the ownership marker, blocks movement. A rule banning entry to enemy-controlled territory would need to be stated separately.

Control could instead provide production, access to an action, or another effect. When control is checked determines whether a transfer happens in time for that benefit. Here it is checked at phase end. Entering an area awards no immediate points, and moving does not repeatedly pay its value.

Retained markers record ownership after departure but add state that must be maintained correctly. Requiring presence removes that historical record while making departure relinquish control. Choose according to the relationship the design needs and whether players can read it clearly on the table.

## Check: does an empty area have no controller?

In the original version, A moves to Square and B waits. May we withhold Cove's 2 points because nobody stands there? **No.** Its ownership marker still belongs to A, so A scores 3. Only the presence-required version gives A just 1.

Can A move directly from Cove to Hill on the first action? **No.** They are not adjacent, and Hill contains B's person. Neither control rule changes those movement restrictions.

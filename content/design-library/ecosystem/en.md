# Shared waters: the condition looks restored, but are consequences still pending?

Some actions yield benefits now and effects next round. A favorable snapshot can omit changes already committed to the system. This develops our shared-water setting by comparing different orders of the same two collection and two restoration actions.

## Two action rounds, then one final settlement

A and B jointly manage fictional waters. Clarity H starts at its maximum of 4, goods at 0, with no pending load. At the start of each round, subtract last round's queued load: each unit reduces H by 1, to a minimum of 0. Clear that load, then let A and B each act once, in order.

Collect to gain 3 goods and queue 2 load for the next round; restore to add 2 H, up to 4; or wait. Each uses one opportunity with no other cost. Everything is public and deterministic. Goods cannot buy actions. H reaching 0 does not end the episode; restoration remains possible.

Play only two action rounds. Then **settle the remaining load at the start of round three, without player actions in that round**. Final goods of at least 6 and H of at least 3 mean shared success; otherwise failure. Benefits are pooled, with no private scoring.

Clarity, load, and their quantities are fictional labels and parameters, not real water-quality or recovery measures.

## Restore first, collect later: the apparent 4 is not final

Both restore in round one, but H is already 4, so nothing accumulates. No load arrives at the start of round two. Both then collect, reaching 6 goods. H temporarily remains 4, with 4 load pending.

At the start of round three that load reduces H from 4 to 0. Final result: **goods 6, H 0, failure**. The round-two value cannot be used as the ending result while the queue remains unresolved.

## Collect first, restore later: the same actions in another window

Reset. Both collect in round one, reaching goods 6, H 4, and queued load 4. At the start of round two, H falls to 0. A restores it to 2; B restores it to 4. No new load is queued, so round three subtracts nothing.

| Order | H after round-two actions | Pending load | Final H | Goods and result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Restore, then collect | 4 | 4 | 0 | 6, failure |
| Collect, then restore | 4 | 0 | 4 | 6, success |

Each sequence uses two collections and two restorations. Even the post-action H values match. The difference is which effects have already occurred and which remain pending. The second finishes with **goods 6, H 4, success**.

## Delay is an executable relationship

Collection changes inventory immediately and the environment through a queue. Restoration affects current H without storing protection beyond its maximum. Timing therefore has a specific role. Unlike the general environmental article, this episode has no stock-triggered natural replenishment.

It does not establish that causing an impact before repairing it is generally preferable. The result depends on capped restoration, immediate repair, fixed effects, and continuation at H 0. Change any of these and recalculate; do not transfer the conclusion to an actual ecosystem.

## Check and model boundary

How would the first sequence be classified if it ended after the round-two actions without settling the queue?

**It would be incorrectly recorded as success: goods 6, H 4.** That is not the current ending rule. The final settlement fulfills consequences registered during collection; it is not a penalty invented after play.

This episode has one environmental indicator, without species, spatial variation, or real timescales. Ask readers to show current H and pending load together, then observe whether they overlook the latter. No human behavior or ecological experiments are reported.

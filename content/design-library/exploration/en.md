# Exploration: learn about the obstacle, or prepare to cross it?

Exploration can involve learning, recording, or completing a survey without claiming territory. In this original episode, one surveyor faces two initially unknown sampling stations. Discovering an obstacle does not remove it: knowing where to go and having the ability to proceed offer different help.

## Two stations and five actions

Camp C connects separately to left station L and right station R. Each connection is bidirectional; L and R do not connect directly. Start at C with no sample or rope kit. Each station has one sample. Exactly one has a fissure and one is clear, initially unrevealed. The map, samples, rules, and one-of-each relationship are public. There are no dice.

Carry at most one sample. Take at most five actions, each choosing one legal option: move along a connection; survey one station from C to reveal its condition; collect a rope kit at C; sample at the current station; submit a carried sample at C; or wait. Each costs one action and nothing else. Entering a station immediately reveals its condition without an extra action.

Sampling at the fissure requires the kit; sampling at the clear station does not. The kit is permanent, occupies no sample capacity, and can be collected only once. Survey only an unrevealed station. Sampling transfers its sample into your hand; only submission adds it to the record. Returning to camp does not submit automatically. Reselect an unavailable action without spending it.

Submitting one sample wins immediately; otherwise lose after action five. There is no ownership, selling, or individual scoring. For the analysis below, the fixed answer is **L has the fissure, R is clear**. Readers are examining a disclosed example, not taking a blind test.

## Go straight left: is there time after discovery?

| Action | Choice | New state |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | C to L | Reveal fissure; cannot sample without kit |
| 2 | L to C | Back empty-handed |
| 3 | Collect kit | Equipped, without sample |
| 4 | C to L | Now able to sample |
| 5 | Sample at L | Sample carried but not submitted; failure |

Discovery provided information but no free equipment, return journey, or submission. The deadline expires without a submitted result, although a survey did take place.

## What can an earlier survey change?

Reset to the same position and answer. Action one surveys L from C, revealing the fissure. The one-of-each rule also establishes that R is clear. Move to R on action two, sample on three, return on four, and submit on five: success.

Another complete success uses the kit first, then moves to L, samples, returns, and submits, again in five actions. One opening buys information that guides selection; the other buys capability for either station. Equal success here does not imply that every exploration game should begin with surveying.

## Which relationship expresses exploration?

The surveyor's actions distinguish learning, preparing, entering, and recording. Unknown obstacles and limited return time create pressure. Information changes route judgment without completing the task. Equipment changes legal actions without revealing an unvisited station.

Revealing both stations initially changes the basis for choice while leaving the map and movement intact. Conversely, adding a face-down card does not automatically create meaningful exploration: specify what is revealed, when it can matter, and what obtaining it displaces.

## Simplifications and the next observation

The stations are fictional sampling installations, not real territories, land rights, or outdoor safety conditions. Obstacles are fixed, without weather, sample quality, or equipment reliability. The kit rules are not practical expedition instructions.

Observe whether readers mistake returning for submitting, and whether they distinguish information from capability. There are no human expedition results, measured learning outcomes, or random success rates here.

## Check: the final action only gets you home

After action four you hold a sample at a station. Action five returns to C. Is that success?

**No.** The sample remains in hand, with no sixth action available to submit it. Making arrival submit automatically would alter the budget and requires a separate variant comparison.

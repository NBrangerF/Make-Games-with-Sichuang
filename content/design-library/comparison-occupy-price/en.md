# Exclusive or shared for a fee: what can the next player still choose?

When a space is occupied, later players might be excluded or allowed to pay for entry. The first difference concerns which actions are legal. To compare the rules, also establish where the fee comes from, who receives it, and what remains after payment.

This original printing-workshop phase uses two workers, two coins, and a score sheet. Follow the sequence before judging the alternatives.

## Fix the shared conditions

A and B each have one worker, one coin, and 0 points. Everything is public; the shared bank starts with no coins. A acts first, then B. Each must place their worker on a legal space and immediately resolve its effect once:

- Printing bench: gain 4 points.
- Proofreading desk: gain 2 points. It is always available, with no capacity limit.

Placement has no additional cost. Workers remain on their spaces throughout this phase. Each player acts only once, with no borrowing, point conversion, or extra actions. After B completes the action, each remaining coin adds 1 point and the phase ends. We compare phase totals without declaring the winner of an unspecified larger game.

Only the printing bench's entry rule differs between versions. The desk, turn order, and end scoring remain identical.

## Version one: one worker at the printing bench

A uses the bench, immediately gains 4 points, and retains one coin. The bench is full. B cannot use it, so B must go to the desk for 2 points, also retaining one coin.

End scoring produces A `4 + 1 = 5` and B `2 + 1 = 3`. Each still holds one coin; the bank has none. One worker occupies the bench and the other the desk.

B is not freely choosing between 4 points and 2. By B's turn, the 4-point space is illegal. Identify that restriction first.

## Version two: later arrivals pay one coin to share

Replace only the bench's entry rule. An empty bench is free. If a worker is already there, a later arrival must first pay one coin they hold to the bank, then place and gain 4 points. Worker count no longer limits entry, and the existing worker remains. A player without the coin cannot choose the bench or collect points before paying.

A again uses the bench first, gains 4 points, and retains one coin. B now has two legal choices:

| B's choice | Immediate points | Coins B retains | B's phase total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Use the desk for free | 2 | 1 | 3 |
| Pay one coin to share the bench | 4 | 0 | 4 |

Follow the second row. Final totals are A 5 and B 4, with both workers at the bench. A retains one coin, the bank has received one, and B has none. B gains 2 additional points from the action but gives up the coin's 1 point, so the net increase is only 1.

Compared with version one, this specified sequence changes B's position from unable to enter to able to pay for entry, then raises B's total from 3 to 4. The bench's extra 2 points are not the net gain.

## Paid access can still be unavailable

Change only B's starting coins to 0. In version two, B cannot pay the one-coin fee and must use the desk. Final totals become A 5 and B 2, with the bank still empty. Version one gives the same result from this starting position.

Allowing paid sharing does not guarantee that every player has the option. Check the resources held before placement. If players with fewer resources should also have access, specify another way to obtain those resources or enter the space.

Here, A should choose the bench to maximize A's own phase score. The example does not provide a complicated first-player tradeoff. It examines later access and its cost, without establishing which version is more enjoyable or fairer over a longer game.

## The recipient of the fee matters too

Restore one coin per player and keep paid sharing. Now change the recipient from the bank to the owner of the worker already at the bench. B pays one coin to A.

Again, A prints first and B pays to print. A now holds two coins, finishing on `4 + 2 = 6`; B still finishes on 4. The bank has none. The choices and payment amount are unchanged, but the first occupant's reward rises from 5 to 6.

This is a separate rule change, not an unstated part of version two. Directing the fee to the first occupant can make early placement a way to obtain income. Whether that serves the design depends on what you want players to compete over.

## Check: does a higher fee merely mean paying more?

Return to version two, with fees paid to the bank and one coin per player. Raise the sharing fee to two coins. A has already entered the empty bench for free. Can B still choose it, and what does B score?

**No. B uses the desk and finishes on 3.** One coin does not satisfy the advance-payment condition. Printing awards points, not coins available to pay the fee. This example has no loan or exchange rule.

The price increase first makes an action illegal, then changes the outcome. When comparing prices, examine both how much is paid and who can still afford entry.
